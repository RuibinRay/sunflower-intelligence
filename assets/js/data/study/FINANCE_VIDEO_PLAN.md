# Finance school: the lesson video plan

Generated by `node tools/study-video-plan.mjs` from the storyboards and `video-plan.js`; don't edit by hand.

Every built finance lesson has a video that narrates the whole lesson in its page order: the opening and objectives,
every paragraph, equation, table and figure of “The idea”, “In depth” and “In practice”, each formula card symbol by
symbol, each worked example step by step, each interactive (with a pause-and-try prompt), each common mistake and
every summary point. `tests/video.test.mjs` fails if any of those is missing from a lesson's narration.

The next layer, lesson by lesson, is an authored opening chapter: a script written for the ear that acts out the
lesson's central moment with a custom stage visual. It replaces the generated chapter it retells, and every number
it says is recomputed in its `checks`.

**Now:** 77 lessons, 35 h 45 min of narration in all (about 28 minutes a lesson at 2.6 words a second). Authored openings written: 3 of 77.

**Stage visuals:**
- `timeline`: Amounts on a dated line, moved one period at a time (built).
- `growth`: Bars that compound or shrink period by period, with the factor on each step.
- `equation`: Assets on one side, liabilities and equity on the other, kept level as each transaction lands.
- `ledger`: A journal entry posting to T-accounts, then the balances carried to a trial balance.
- `bridge`: A waterfall from one total to another (net income to cash, enterprise value to equity).
- `tree`: A ratio split into its drivers, each branch lighting as it is computed.
- `curve`: A line, curve or frontier traced point by point (yield curve, efficient frontier, NPV profile).
- `payoff`: Payoff and profit lines that move as the inputs change.

**Order of work:** the visuals that unlock the most lessons first (bridge, curve, growth, ledger), then the openings
course by course in study order, F00 to F06; each new F07–F13 lesson ships with its video and opening.

### F12 Managerial Accounting and Control (planned course)

The numbers managers use inside the business: cost behaviour, product costing, budgets, variances and performance measures that steer decisions.

1. *Costs and decisions:* Cost behaviour; Cost–volume–profit analysis; Relevant costs for decisions.
2. *Product costing:* Job-order and process costing; Activity-based costing.
3. *Planning and control:* The master budget; Flexible budgets and variances; Standard costing; Responsibility accounting and transfer pricing; The balanced scorecard.

Each lesson gets its generated video when it is written, and an authored opening in the same review pass.

### F13 Financial Markets, Institutions and Ethics (planned course)

How banks, insurers, exchanges and regulators work, why markets are efficient and when they are not, what behavioural finance adds, how currencies are priced, and the ethical standards of the profession.

1. *Institutions:* Banks and bank runs; Central banks and monetary policy; Insurance; Exchanges, brokers and clearing.
2. *How markets behave:* Market efficiency; Behavioural finance; Bubbles and crises.
3. *Currencies, rules and ethics:* Foreign exchange and interest-rate parity; Regulation; Ethics and professional standards.

Each lesson gets its generated video when it is written, and an authored opening in the same review pass.

### F08 Derivatives and Risk Management (planned course)

Forwards, futures, swaps and options: how they are priced by no arbitrage, and how a business or a portfolio uses them to hedge and to measure risk.

1. *Forwards, futures and swaps:* Why firms hedge; Forwards and futures; Pricing by no arbitrage; Swaps.
2. *Options:* Calls, puts, payoffs and profits; Put–call parity; Binomial pricing; Black–Scholes–Merton and the Greeks.
3. *Managing risk:* Hedging with derivatives; Margin, leverage and short selling; Value at Risk and stress tests.

Each lesson gets its generated video when it is written, and an authored opening in the same review pass.

### F09 Fixed Income and Credit (planned course)

Price and analyse bonds beyond the basics: the yield curve, duration and convexity, credit risk and spreads, and the securitised products built from loans.

1. *Bond pricing:* Bond features and covenants; Yield measures; Spot and forward rates.
2. *Interest-rate risk:* The yield curve and its theories; Duration; Convexity and immunisation.
3. *Credit:* Default, recovery and expected loss; Ratings and spreads; Securitisation; Bond portfolio strategies.

Each lesson gets its generated video when it is written, and an authored opening in the same review pass.

### F10 Valuation, Mergers and Restructuring (planned course)

Value a whole business with intrinsic and relative methods, then the deals that change companies: acquisitions, leveraged buyouts, distress and restructuring.

1. *Valuing a business:* Choosing a valuation approach; Free cash flow to the firm and to equity; Terminal value; Young, cyclical and distressed companies.
2. *Relative and sum-of-the-parts:* Choosing and adjusting multiples; Sum of the parts.
3. *Deals and restructuring:* Mergers and acquisitions: synergies and premiums; Accretion, dilution and deal structure; Leveraged buyouts; Distress, bankruptcy and restructuring.

Each lesson gets its generated video when it is written, and an authored opening in the same review pass.

### F11 Alternative Investments (planned course)

Private equity, venture capital, hedge funds, real estate, private credit and commodities: how each makes money, how its returns are measured, and what the reported numbers hide.

1. *Private markets:* What makes an investment alternative; Fund structures, fees and carried interest; Private equity; Venture capital.
2. *Measuring private returns:* IRR, multiples and public market equivalent; Smoothed returns and biases.
3. *Other alternatives:* Hedge fund strategies; Real estate; Private credit, commodities and infrastructure; Due diligence and allocation.

Each lesson gets its generated video when it is written, and an authored opening in the same review pass.
