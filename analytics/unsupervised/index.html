Find structure without labels: groups of similar rows and the few directions that explain most of the variation.

No answer column here. These methods look for structure on their own: groups of rows that sit close together, and the few directions that carry most of the variation. Columns are put on a common scale first, so a column measured in thousands doesn’t drown one measured in units.

Your data

Choose a table, then the numeric columns to look for structure in. Every section below uses this choice. A table you loaded under Explore appears here too.

Columns to use

k-means

k-means splits the rows into the number of groups you choose, moving each group’s centre until every row sits with its nearest centre. The silhouette score says how cleanly the groups separate.

—silhouette score (−1 to 1; above 0.5 is a clear structure)

  • k-means always returns the number of groups you ask for, structure or not; a low silhouette says the groups are a convenient cut, not a discovery.
  • It favours round, similar-sized groups and is pulled by outliers. Results are seeded so they repeat.

Hierarchical clustering

Start with every row on its own and keep merging the two closest groups until one remains. Ward’s method merges whichever pair adds least to the spread inside groups. The tree of merges, a dendrogram, shows every grouping at once: cut it lower for more groups, higher for fewer.

—of the spread lies between the groups at this cut

    Principal components

    Principal component analysis finds new axes, each a weighted mix of your columns, ordered by how much of the variation they carry. If the first two carry most of it, a flat map of the rows on those two loses little, and the weights say what each axis means.

    Any column with two values; the rows at its higher value are drawn in colour.

    —of the variation is carried by the first two components

    • Working on the correlation matrix treats every column equally, whatever its units.
    • The sign of a component is arbitrary: flipping every weight describes the same axis. Here each is signed so its largest weight is positive.
    • PCA finds straight-line structure only, and a two-valued column contributes little more than its proportion; components summarise variation, they don’t explain it.
