Compare projects

Enter up to three projects’ cash flows: the outlay today as a negative number, then what each year brings back. Every measure is worked out as you type.

Load a classic case

Invented figures, chosen to show where the measures disagree.

  • NPV is the measure to decide on: it is the value a project adds at your cost of capital. The others are summaries that can mislead when projects differ in size or timing.
  • Payback ignores everything after the money comes back; discounted payback ignores less but still stops counting.
  • When the cash-flow signs change more than once there can be several IRRs, or none. MIRR always gives one answer.

NPV profile

The same projects at every discount rate. Each line crosses zero at its IRR; where two lines cross, the projects are worth the same and the ranking flips.

Capital rationing

More good projects than money. Ranking by profitability index is the textbook shortcut; with whole projects it can miss the best combination, so this checks every combination too.
