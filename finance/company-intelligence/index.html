Company Intelligence

Upload a company’s workbook, whatever its size, and get a dashboard you can defend: every sheet and row read, every mapping yours to correct, every figure traced to its cell, and nothing invented where the data is silent.

Excel .xls and .xlsx, CSV and TSV, with several sheets and tens of thousands of rows. The file is read in a Web Worker in this browser tab and never uploaded. The page finds the financial statements, long financial records and operational datasets, proposes a mapping for you to check, and then builds the statements, ratios, reconciliation checks and, once you supply what a spreadsheet cannot know, a valuation on the same engine as the Company Valuation Studio.

Workspace

Four steps: bring the data, see what the workbook holds, review the mapping, read the dashboard. A figure is always one of four things, and labelled so: data from your file, derived from it, an assumption you set, or unavailable.

Sources and method

  1. Workbooks are read with SheetJS Community Edition 0.20.3 (Apache-2.0), served from this site. Macros are never run; formulas are not recalculated, their stored results are read, and cells without one are reported.
  2. Ratio definitions: CFA Institute, Financial Statement Analysis; Stobierski, T. (2020), How to determine the financial health of a company, Harvard Business School Online. Return on invested capital: Koller, Goedhart and Wessels (2020), Valuation, 7th ed.
  3. Distress scores: Altman, E. I. (2000). Predicting financial distress of companies: revisiting the Z-score and ZETA models. NYU Stern working paper.
  4. Valuation methods and their sources are listed on the Company Valuation Studio page. The calculation audit is in AUDIT.md.
  5. The sample company, Helio Foods Group, is fictional; its workbooks are generated by tests/make_workbooks.py.
