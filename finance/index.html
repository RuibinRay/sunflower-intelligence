Finance

An obsession with yellow

In Arles, Van Gogh chased a yellow so intense he had to steel himself to reach it. This room chases the business equivalent: the cash a company really produces, and how safely it can keep producing it.

“I had to key myself up a bit to reach the high yellow note…”Vincent van Gogh to Theo, Arles, 24 March 1889

Five pages, in the order a financial decision runs: what a business is worth, which projects to fund, how a share behaves in a portfolio, how much could be lost, and how bonds and options are priced. Results update as you type, and every assumption stays on the page.

  • Valuation

    What a business is worth: the cost of capital, then its cash flows discounted at that rate.

    • Cost of capital (CAPM, WACC)
    • Discounted cash flow with sensitivity
    Open

  • Investment decisions

    Which projects to fund, compared on NPV, IRR, MIRR, payback and profitability index.

    • Capital budgeting for up to three projects
    • NPV profile and crossover rate
    • Capital rationing
    Open

  • Stock analysis

    Return, risk and beta for a share against the market, and the portfolio mix with the best return for its risk.

    • Returns, volatility, drawdown, Sharpe
    • Beta and alpha by CAPM regression
    • Efficient frontier
    Open

  • Risk management

    How much a portfolio could lose on a bad day, whether the model has been right, and how a company’s credit looks.

    • Value at Risk and Expected Shortfall
    • Kupiec backtest
    • Stress scenarios
    • Altman Z-score
    Open

  • Bonds and options

    Bond prices, yields, duration and convexity; option values, Greeks and implied volatility.

    • Bonds
    • Options (Black–Scholes–Merton)
