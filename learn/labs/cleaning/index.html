How the lab works

The definitions behind every number, and what the lab will not do for you.

Summary statistics

The standard deviation is the sample SD, dividing by n − 1. Quartiles use linear interpolation between ordered values: the p-th quantile sits at position (n − 1) × p, counting from 0. This is Hyndman and Fan’s type 7, NumPy’s default and Excel’s QUARTILE.INC. Other software may use another definition and give slightly different quartiles for small samples.

Skewness

The adjusted Fisher–Pearson coefficient G₁ = √(n(n − 1)) ÷ (n − 2) × m₃ ÷ m₂^(3/2), where m₂ and m₃ are the second and third central moments with n in the denominator (the same as Excel’s SKEW). As a rule of thumb, |G₁| above 1 is strong skew; the lab reports skew for columns with at least 20 values and 12 distinct values.

Outlier rules

IQR fences: a value is flagged below Q1 − k × IQR or above Q3 + k × IQR. With k = 1.5 these are the inner fences (mild outliers); k = 3 gives the outer fences (extreme outliers). z-score: z = (x − mean) ÷ SD, flagged when |z| exceeds the threshold (3 by default); the mean and SD include the extreme values themselves, which can hide them in small samples. Modified z-score: M = 0.6745 × (x − median) ÷ MAD, where MAD is the median absolute deviation from the median; the threshold 3.5 is Iglewicz and Hoaglin’s recommendation. Dividing by 0.6745 is the same as multiplying the MAD by 1.4826, which makes it comparable to an SD for normal data. If more than half the values are equal the MAD is 0 and the lab refuses the rule rather than divide by zero.

Types, dates and numbers

A value is a number if it reads as one with the chosen decimal mark; with a decimal point, commas must separate groups of three digits, so “16,50” is never silently read as 1,650. Dates become ISO 8601 (YYYY-MM-DD). In automatic order, a slash date that reads both ways (04/05/2026) is left unchanged and listed; choose day first or month first from evidence. Two-digit years 00–68 mean 2000–2068 and 69–99 mean 1969–1999. Spreadsheet serials use Excel’s 1900 date system, counting days from 30 December 1899, so 46085 is 4 March 2026.

Missing values

Missing completely at random (MCAR): the chance a value is missing is the same for every row. Missing at random (MAR): it depends only on things you observe, such as subscription. Missing not at random (MNAR): it depends on something you don’t observe, often the missing value itself. The lab tests whether missingness differs across the groups of each other column (a chi-square test, reported when p < 0.01 and the rates differ by at least 15 points). A significant result rules out MCAR for that pattern; no test can tell MAR from MNAR.

Leakage

Scaling, imputation and target encoding learn numbers from the data: a mean, a minimum, a group average. For a model, learn them from the training rows only and apply the same numbers to the test rows; otherwise information from the test rows leaks into training and the test score looks better than it should. The lab offers a seeded 70% training subset (or a split column) for this, and its notes say which rows the parameters came from.

Limits. The lab finds problems that break a pattern or a rule; a value can be wrong and still look normal. Spelling suggestions come from spelling alone and must be checked. Issue detection uses rules from each dataset’s data dictionary; for your own data it can only use general checks. It handles one table at a time (no joins) and is built for teaching-sized data: it stays responsive up to a few thousand rows. Every decision is yours, and the report records it.
