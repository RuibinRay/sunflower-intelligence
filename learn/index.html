Learning by doing

Van Gogh was largely self-taught. He worked through drawing courses page by page and copied the painters he admired, long before the sunflowers. Practice that is a little beyond you, repeated before you forget it, is still how skills grow.

    How the courses work

    Complete is not the same as mastered

    A lesson is complete when you have worked its guided problems and scored 80% on its knowledge check, not when you open it. Mastery is estimated separately from your first answers, using Bayesian knowledge tracing, and decays unless you review.

    Worked examples, then practice

    Beginners learn faster from studied examples than from solving problems cold (Sweller and Cooper, 1985), and answering questions strengthens memory more than rereading (Roediger and Karpicke, 2006). Every lesson does both, with hints first and feedback that explains the mistake.

    Spaced review

    Each lesson comes back for review when you are predicted to be about to forget it, using half-life regression (Settles and Meeder, 2016). Questions you got wrong come back too, and the glossary doubles as flashcards.

    Checked, and honest about it

    Every number printed in a lesson is recomputed by a test; journal entries must balance and statements must add up. Each lesson was reviewed for accuracy by a second reader. Sources are listed in every lesson, and anything not yet verified says so.

    Your progress stays with you

    Progress, notes and bookmarks are kept in this browser only: no account, nothing sent anywhere. Clearing your browser’s site data deletes them, so the Notebook lets you download a copy and load it elsewhere.

    What it can’t do

    There is no teacher marking your work: written answers are marked by you against a model answer and a rubric. Answer keys are in the page’s code, as on any static site. The labs’ data and the companies in them are fictional.
