An option is a right without an obligation. Its value comes from what the share might do before expiry, which is why volatility matters so much.

The idea

A call is the right to buy a share at a fixed strike price on or before expiry; a put is the right to sell. At expiry a call is worth max(S − K, 0) and a put max(K − S, 0). The holder pays a premium up front and can never lose more than that.

Before expiry an option is worth more than its payoff today, because the share may still move in the holder’s favour. The Black–Scholes–Merton model prices that from five inputs: share price, strike, time, interest rate and volatility. More volatility raises both calls and puts, since big moves help the holder and losses are capped.

The Greeks measure sensitivities. Delta is the change in option value per $1 move in the share; vega, per point of volatility; theta, the value lost as time passes. Put–call parity ties the two together: a call minus a put behaves like owning the share and owing the present value of the strike. Implied volatility is the volatility that makes the model match a market price.

The formula

C = S × N(d1) − K e−rT × N(d2), d1 = ln(S/K) + (r + σ2/2)Tσ√T, d2 = d1 − σ√T

S, K
share price today and strike price
T
time to expiry in years
r
risk-free rate, continuously compounded
σ
volatility of the share’s returns per year
N(x)
the standard normal cumulative probability; N(d1) is the call’s delta

Put–call parity: C − P = S − K e−rT. Both are the versions for a share that pays no dividend before expiry; the tool also takes a dividend yield.

The common mistake

Forgetting to discount the strike in put–call parity

Writing P = C − S + K gives 3.80 − 50 + 52 = $5.80, overpricing the put by $1.03. The strike is paid at expiry, so parity uses its present value, K e−rT.

Remember

  • A call is the right to buy at the strike, a put the right to sell; the most a holder can lose is the premium.
  • Higher volatility makes both calls and puts more valuable. Value change ≈ delta × share move.
  • Call − put = S − K e−rT for European options on a share without dividends.
