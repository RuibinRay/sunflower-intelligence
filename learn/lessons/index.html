In 1880 Van Gogh taught himself to draw by copying the sheets of Charles Bargue’s drawing books one at a time; “now I’ve finished the 60 sheets,” he wrote to Theo that September. These lessons are plates in the same spirit: each is short, works an example step by step, and ends with you doing it yourself.

How the lessons work

A worked example first

Beginners who study worked examples before solving problems learn faster and make fewer errors on similar problems than those who start by solving (Sweller and Cooper, 1985).

Then you try

Each lesson’s calculator lets you change the inputs and see the answer move, the step from following an example to using the idea.

Then you’re tested

Retrieving an idea strengthens memory more than rereading it (Roediger and Karpicke, 2006), and a large review rates practice testing and spaced practice the most useful study techniques (Dunlosky et al., 2013). The questions update your mastery, and Practise brings the skill back before you forget it.

Short pieces

Lessons are split into small labelled parts, without decoration, so working memory goes on the idea (Mayer, 2009).
