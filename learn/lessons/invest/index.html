NPV, IRR, payback and the profitability index often agree. When they don’t, NPV is the one to trust, and knowing why is most of capital budgeting.

The idea

The net present value of a project is every cash flow discounted at the cost of capital, outlay included. A positive NPV is the value the project adds in today’s dollars, so fund it; between alternatives, fund the one with the highest NPV.

The internal rate of return is the discount rate at which NPV is exactly zero. For one ordinary project it gives the same yes or no as NPV. Between alternatives it can mislead: a percentage ignores size and rewards early cash. And when cash-flow signs change more than once, as with a mine that needs cleaning up, there can be several IRRs.

The payback period counts years until the outlay comes back and ignores everything after. The profitability index, the present value per dollar invested, helps when the budget is short, but with projects that can’t be split, ranking by it can miss the best combination.

The formula

NPV = CF0 + CF1(1 + r) + … + CFN(1 + r)N, PI = NPV + outlayoutlay

CF0
the outlay today, a negative number
CFt
cash flow at the end of year t
r
the cost of capital for projects of this risk
IRR
the discount rate r at which NPV is exactly zero
PI
profitability index: present value of the inflows per dollar of outlay
crossover rate
the rate at which two projects have the same NPV: the IRR of the difference between their cash flows

The common mistake

Ranking alternatives by IRR

Choosing B because 18.3% beats 15.4% gives up $31.6k of value. IRR answers “what rate would make this break even?”, not “which project adds the most?”. Use it to accept or reject a single ordinary project; use NPV to choose.

Remember

  • Fund positive-NPV projects; between alternatives, the highest NPV.
  • IRR and payback favour early cash and small projects. Where two NPV lines cross, the ranking flips.
  • More than one sign change can mean more than one IRR: use NPV, or MIRR. With a fixed budget and whole projects, check combinations rather than ranking by PI.
