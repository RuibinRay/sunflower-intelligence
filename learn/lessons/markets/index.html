  4. Choosing markets: attractiveness and reach

Rank countries on what makes a market worth entering, on one scale and with weights you choose, then weigh how attractive each is against how hard it is to reach.

The idea

Market measures come in different units: GDP in dollars, growth in percent, population in people. An attractiveness score puts each on a common 0–100 scale across the countries you compare, lowest 0 and highest 100, then adds them up with your weights.

Size needs a log scale. GDP runs from a few billion dollars to tens of trillions, so on a raw scale the biggest economies sit near 100 and everyone else near 0. Growth is a compound annual growth rate: from $200bn to $300bn in five years is (300 ÷ 200)1/5 − 1 = 8.4% a year, not 10%.

Scores are relative: change the set of countries and every score can move. And attractive is not the same as reachable. Plotting attractiveness against reach, closeness to your home market, gives a GE–McKinsey-style grid: high on both is where to grow first; high on only one calls for selectivity.

The formula

scaled = 100 × x − lowesthighest − lowest, score = Σ weight × scaled, CAGR = (end ÷ start)1/years − 1

x
a country’s measure; for GDP, its natural logarithm
weight
how much each measure counts; weights are divided by their total so they add to 1
CAGR
compound annual growth rate
reach
100 next door, 0 on the far side of the planet

The market map also caps each measure at its 5th and 95th percentiles before scaling, so one extreme country can’t squash the rest.

The common mistake

Scaling GDP without logs

On a raw scale B’s size score is (400 − 50) ÷ (2,000 − 50) × 100 = 18 instead of 56, its total falls to 24, and the tiny market A, at 40, overtakes it. The largest economy sets the scale and everyone else looks the same size.

Remember

  • Put every measure on 0–100 across the countries in view, size on a log scale, then weight them. Change the set and the scores change.
  • Growth is compound: CAGR = (end ÷ start)1/years − 1. Measured in US dollars it includes inflation and currency moves.
  • Weigh attractiveness against reach: strong on both, grow there first; strong on only one, be selective.
