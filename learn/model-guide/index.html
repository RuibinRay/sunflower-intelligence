Five parts: a chooser that tells you which valuation model fits a company and why, four models you can take apart (a discounted cash flow, the cost of capital, diversification and a regression line), and exercises with hints and instant feedback. Every number is computed by the same engines as the site’s tools, on a fictional company unless you bring your own.

Which model should I use?

Describe the company. The guide ranks eight valuation models from “recommended” to “unsuitable” and says why, including the cases where the textbook method is the wrong one.

A discounted cash flow, step by step

Four steps: forecast the cash, shrink each year’s cash for the wait, value everything after the forecast, then take off debt to get to the shares.

The cost of capital as a blend

Shareholders and lenders each want a return. WACC is the average, weighted by how much of the money each provided, with the tax saving on interest taken off.

Why diversification works

Mix two shares and the risk of the mix is less than the average of their risks, unless they move in lockstep. Slide the correlation and the weight.

Fitting a line by least squares

Try to beat the computer: move the line, watch the squared misses add up, then let least squares find the smallest total. More data narrows the uncertainty about the slope.

Guided practice

Fresh numbers each time. Ask for a hint when stuck; the worked answer appears after you check.
