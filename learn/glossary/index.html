4

4Cs

Robert Lauterborn’s (1990) buyer’s-side alternative to the 4Ps: consumer wants and needs, cost to satisfy, convenience to buy, and communication.

M02 · The marketing mix and customer value

4Ps

Product, price, place and promotion: the four headings E. Jerome McCarthy (1960) used to organise the marketing mix.

M02 · The marketing mix and customer value

7

7Ps

The 4Ps plus people, process and physical evidence, proposed by Booms and Bitner (1981) for marketing services.

M02 · The marketing mix and customer value

A

Account balance

The difference between an account’s total debits and total credits, including any balance brought forward, stated on the side of the larger total.

F01 · Debits, credits and normal balances

Accounting

An information system that identifies the economic events of an organisation, records them in money and communicates the results to the people who make decisions about it.

F01 · What accounting is and why businesses need it

Accounting equation

Assets = liabilities + equity: the business’s resources equal the claims of creditors and owners on them.

F01 · The accounting equation

Accrual

A timing difference in which revenue or an expense is recognised before the cash is received or paid. Meanwhile the amount is a receivable or a payable.

F01 · Accrual versus cash accounting

Accrual basis

A way of keeping accounts that records revenue when it is earned and expenses when they are incurred, whenever the cash is received or paid. Financial statements under US GAAP and IFRS use it.

F01 · Accrual versus cash accounting

Accrued expense

An expense incurred in the period but not yet paid or recorded, such as wages or interest owed. It is recorded with a payable.

F01 · Adjusting entries

Accrued revenue

Revenue earned in the period but not yet received or recorded, for example work done but not yet invoiced. It is recorded with a receivable.

F01 · Adjusting entries

Accumulated depreciation

All the depreciation recorded on an asset since it was acquired, held in a contra-asset account with a credit balance.

F01 · Adjusting entries

Adjusted trial balance

A list of every account and its balance after the adjusting entries have been posted, used to prepare the financial statements.

F01 · Adjusting entries

Adjusting entry

A journal entry made at the end of a period to bring an account up to date, so that revenue and expenses are recognised in the right period. It involves at least one income statement account and one balance sheet account, and never Cash.

F01 · Adjusting entries

Aggregation

Collapsing groups of rows into one summary row per group by applying a function such as sum, mean or count. It moves a table to a coarser granularity.

B02 · Transforming and reshaping data

Audit

An independent examination of financial statements, after which the auditor gives an opinion on whether they are presented fairly, in all material respects, in accordance with the applicable financial reporting framework.

F01 · What accounting is and why businesses need it

B

Balance-column account

A ledger account laid out with date, explanation, reference, debit, credit and balance columns, showing the balance after every posting. Also called a running-balance or three-column account.

F01 · Posting to the general ledger

Binning

Replacing a numeric value with the interval (bin) it falls in. Bin edges can be equal-width, equal-frequency (quantiles) or set by a business rule.

B02 · Transforming and reshaping data

Book value

An asset’s cost minus its accumulated depreciation; called the carrying amount under IFRS.

F01 · Adjusting entries

Break-even point

The volume (or revenue) at which total contribution equals fixed costs, so profit is zero: fixed costs ÷ contribution margin per unit.

M02 · Pricing approaches

Business key

The column or columns that identify one real-world thing in the business’s own terms, such as an order number or booking reference.

B02 · Duplicate records

C

Cash basis

A way of keeping accounts that records revenue when cash is received and expenses when cash is paid.

F01 · Accrual versus cash accounting

Closing entries

Journal entries made at the end of an accounting period that transfer the balances of the revenue, expense and dividend accounts to Retained Earnings, leaving those accounts at zero.

F01 · Closing entries

Compensating error

Two or more errors whose effects on the debit and credit totals cancel out, so the trial balance still balances.

F01 · The trial balance

Competition-based pricing

Setting a price mainly from competitors’ prices: charging the going rate in markets for similar products, or bidding against expected rival bids in sealed-bid contracts.

M02 · Pricing approaches

Composite key

A primary key made of two or more columns, such as order_id and line together in a line-item report.

B02 · Datasets: rows, columns, records and variables

Compound entry

A journal entry with more than one debit, more than one credit, or both. Its debit total still equals its credit total.

F01 · Journal entries

Conceptual framework

A coherent set of concepts underlying accounting standards: the objective of financial reporting, the qualities that make information useful, and the definition, recognition and measurement of the elements of financial statements.

F01 · What accounting is and why businesses need it

Continuous variable

A numerical variable that can take any value within a range, at least in principle, usually a measurement such as weight or time.

B02 · Datasets: rows, columns, records and variables

Contributed capital

Equity supplied by owners in exchange for shares, recorded in Common Stock (share capital under IFRS).

F01 · The accounting equation

Contribution margin

Price received minus variable cost per unit: what each unit contributes towards fixed costs and then profit. As a share of price it is the contribution margin ratio.

M02 · Pricing approaches

Control account

A general-ledger account whose balance must equal the total of a subsidiary ledger, such as Accounts Receivable for the customer accounts.

F01 · Posting to the general ledger

Cost-plus pricing

Setting a price by adding a markup, a fixed percentage of the unit cost, to that cost.

M02 · Pricing approaches

Credit

The right side of an account, or an amount entered there (abbreviated Cr). A credit increases liabilities, equity and revenue and decreases assets, expenses and dividends.

F01 · Debits, credits and normal balances

Customer value

The customer’s judgement of the benefits of an offer relative to its total cost in money, time, effort and risk, compared with the alternatives.

M02 · The marketing mix and customer value

D

Data dictionary

A document recording, for each column of a dataset, its meaning, level of measurement, type, unit, allowed values, rule for blanks and business rules.

B02 · Datasets: rows, columns, records and variables

Data leakage

Using information when building a model that would not be available when the model makes real predictions, such as test-set values used to fit a preparation step. It makes performance estimates too optimistic.

B02 · Normalisation and standardisation

Dataset

A collection of values organised so that each value describes one characteristic of one thing; usually a table of rows and columns.

B02 · Datasets: rows, columns, records and variables

Debit

The left side of an account, or an amount entered there (abbreviated Dr). A debit increases assets, expenses and dividends and decreases liabilities, equity and revenue.

F01 · Debits, credits and normal balances

Deduplication

Finding records that describe the same thing and reducing each group to a single record, or merging them, according to stated rules.

B02 · Duplicate records

Deferral

A timing difference in which cash is received or paid before the revenue or expense is recognised. Meanwhile the amount is a liability (unearned revenue) or an asset (prepaid expense).

F01 · Accrual versus cash accounting

Depreciation

The systematic allocation of the cost of a long-lived asset, less its residual value, over its useful life. It is not a measure of market value.

F01 · Adjusting entries

Derived variable

A new variable calculated from other variables of the same row, such as expected total = quantity × unit price − discount, or nights = check-out date − check-in date.

B02 · Transforming and reshaping data

Discrete variable

A numerical variable that takes separate values with gaps between them, usually a count, such as packs ordered.

B02 · Datasets: rows, columns, records and variables

Dividends

Distributions of profit to a corporation’s shareholders. They reduce equity but are not an expense.

F01 · The accounting equation

Double-entry bookkeeping

The system of recording every transaction in at least two accounts with equal debits and credits, so that the accounting equation stays in balance.

F01 · Debits, credits and normal balances

Dual aspect

The principle that every transaction affects at least two items in the accounting equation, so it stays in balance.

F01 · The accounting equation

Duplicate record

A row that describes the same real-world entity or event as another row in the same dataset.

B02 · Duplicate records

E

Economic entity assumption

The assumption that a business is separate from its owners and from other businesses, so its records contain only its own transactions and events.

F01 · What accounting is and why businesses need it

Edit distance

The smallest number of single-character insertions, deletions and substitutions needed to turn one string into another; also called Levenshtein distance.

B02 · Duplicate records

Error of commission

An amount recorded on the correct side but in the wrong account of the same type, such as rent posted to utilities expense or an invoice posted to the wrong customer.

F01 · The trial balance

Error of omission

A transaction left out of the records entirely. Both sides are missing, so the trial balance still balances.

F01 · The trial balance

Error of original entry

A wrong amount recorded in the journal and posted to both accounts, so debits still equal credits.

F01 · The trial balance

Error of principle

An amount recorded on the correct side but in the wrong type of account, such as a repair (an expense) debited to equipment (an asset).

F01 · The trial balance

Exact duplicate

A row whose values are identical to another row’s in every column.

B02 · Duplicate records

Executory contract

A contract in which neither party has yet performed. It is not recorded in the accounts until one side acts, for example by paying or delivering.

F01 · The accounting equation

Expanded accounting equation

Assets = liabilities + common stock + beginning retained earnings + revenue − expenses − dividends.

F01 · The accounting equation

Expense recognition principle

Expenses are recognised in the period in which they are incurred, usually the period whose revenue they helped to earn. Often called the matching principle.

F01 · Accrual versus cash accounting

F

Faithful representation

The quality of information that depicts what it claims to depict: complete, neutral and free from error. One of the two fundamental qualitative characteristics.

F01 · What accounting is and why businesses need it

FASB

The Financial Accounting Standards Board: the independent, private-sector, not-for-profit body, established in 1973, that sets US GAAP and is recognised by the SEC as the accounting standard setter for public companies.

F01 · What accounting is and why businesses need it

Feature scaling

Converting numeric variables to comparable, unitless scales before they are used together in an analysis or model.

B02 · Normalisation and standardisation

Financial accounting

The branch of accounting that prepares general-purpose financial statements for outside users, such as investors and lenders, following accounting standards (US GAAP or IFRS).

F01 · What accounting is and why businesses need it

Financial statements

Structured reports of a business’s performance, financial position and cash flows: the income statement, statement of changes in equity, balance sheet and statement of cash flows, together with their notes.

F01 · What accounting is and why businesses need it

Fixed cost

A cost whose total stays the same over a period whatever the volume, within the range of activity planned: rent, salaries, listing fees, a committed promotion budget.

M02 · Pricing approaches

Full disclosure principle

The principle that the financial statements and their notes should include all the information a reader needs to understand them.

F01 · What accounting is and why businesses need it

Full outer join

A join that keeps every row of both tables, matched where possible.

B02 · Transforming and reshaping data

Fuzzy matching

Comparing values by how similar they are rather than whether they are exactly equal, to find records that probably refer to the same thing.

B02 · Duplicate records

G

GAAP

Generally accepted accounting principles: the body of standards and conventions that businesses in a jurisdiction follow in financial reporting. US GAAP is set by the FASB.

F01 · What accounting is and why businesses need it

General journal

The all-purpose journal, in which any kind of transaction can be recorded. Businesses that also keep special journals use it for entries that fit none of them, such as adjusting and closing entries.

F01 · Journal entries

General ledger

The collection of all a business’s accounts, one for each account in the chart of accounts, showing every amount posted to each account and its balance.

F01 · Posting to the general ledger

Going concern assumption

The assumption that, unless there is evidence to the contrary, a business will continue operating for the foreseeable future.

F01 · What accounting is and why businesses need it

Granularity

The level of detail that one row of a table represents, such as one order, one customer or one region in one month.

B02 · Transforming and reshaping data

H

Historical cost principle

The principle that assets are recorded at what they cost to acquire and get ready for use, and generally kept at that amount rather than updated to current value.

F01 · What accounting is and why businesses need it

I

IASB

The International Accounting Standards Board: the independent standard-setting body within the IFRS Foundation that develops IFRS Accounting Standards. It was set up in 2001.

F01 · What accounting is and why businesses need it

IFRS

International Financial Reporting Standards (formally IFRS Accounting Standards), written by the IASB. Each jurisdiction decides whether and for which companies to require them.

F01 · What accounting is and why businesses need it

Imputation

Replacing a missing value with a substitute value, such as a mean, a group median, a category such as “Unknown” or a model’s prediction.

B02 · Missing values

Income Summary

A temporary account used only during closing. Revenue and expenses are closed into it, so its balance equals net income or net loss; it is then closed to Retained Earnings. It never appears on a financial statement.

F01 · Closing entries

Inner join

A join that keeps only the rows whose key appears in both tables.

B02 · Transforming and reshaping data

Interquartile range

The third quartile minus the first quartile: the width of the middle half of the data (IQR).

B02 · Outliers and impossible values

Interval variable

A numerical variable on which differences are meaningful but zero is an arbitrary point, so ratios are not, such as a calendar date or a temperature in °C.

B02 · Datasets: rows, columns, records and variables

J

Join

Combining two tables by matching the values of a key column, so that each row gains the matching columns of the other table.

B02 · Transforming and reshaping data

Journal

A record of transactions in date order, each written as a complete entry with its debits, credits and explanation. Also called the book of original entry.

F01 · Journal entries

Journal entry

The record of one transaction in a journal: the date, the accounts and amounts debited and credited, and a short explanation.

F01 · Journal entries

Journalising

Recording transactions in a journal.

F01 · Journal entries

L

Left join

A join that keeps every row of the left table, leaving the right table’s columns missing where there is no match.

B02 · Transforming and reshaping data

Listwise deletion

Dropping every row that has a missing value in any variable an analysis uses; also called complete-case analysis.

B02 · Missing values

Long format

A layout in which that variable has a column of its own and each combination of values has its own row.

B02 · Datasets: rows, columns, records and variables

M

Managerial accounting

The branch of accounting that prepares internal reports to help managers plan, control and decide. It follows no externally imposed rules and often uses nonfinancial measures. Also called management accounting.

F01 · What accounting is and why businesses need it

MAR

Missing at random: whether a value is missing depends only on recorded variables; within groups defined by them it is unrelated to the missing value itself.

B02 · Missing values

Margin

Profit per unit as a percentage of price: (price − cost) ÷ price. Also called margin on price or, in some texts, markup on selling price.

M02 · Pricing approaches

Marketing

The activity of creating, communicating, delivering and exchanging offerings that have value for customers, clients, partners and society (a paraphrase of the American Marketing Association’s definition).

M02 · The marketing mix and customer value

Marketing mix

The set of controllable decisions a business combines to create, communicate, deliver and capture value for its target market.

M02 · The marketing mix and customer value

Markup

Profit per unit as a percentage of cost: (price − cost) ÷ cost.

M02 · Pricing approaches

Masking

The effect in which outliers inflate the mean and standard deviation used to detect them, so that they, or other outliers, no longer look extreme.

B02 · Outliers and impossible values

Materiality

Information is material if omitting or misstating it could influence the decisions of the people relying on the report. It depends on the size and nature of the item in a particular business; there is no universal threshold.

F01 · What accounting is and why businesses need it

MCAR

Missing completely at random: whether a value is missing is unrelated to any data, recorded or not.

B02 · Missing values

Mean imputation

Filling every missing value in a column with the mean of the observed values; it keeps the mean, shrinks the spread and weakens relationships.

B02 · Missing values

Median absolute deviation

The median of the absolute distances of the values from their median (MAD); a robust measure of spread.

B02 · Outliers and impossible values

Melt

Reshaping from wide to long format, also called unpivoting: several columns become one column of former headers and one column of values.

B02 · Transforming and reshaping data

Min–max normalisation

Rescaling a variable as (x − minimum) ÷ (maximum − minimum), so the training data run from 0 to 1.

B02 · Normalisation and standardisation

Missing value

A cell where a value should exist but was not recorded, whether it appears as a blank, a code such as NA or −999, a text stand-in such as “unknown”, or an impossible value.

B02 · Missing values

Missing-value indicator

A 0/1 column that records which values were missing before imputation, kept for auditing and as a possible model input.

B02 · Missing values

MNAR

Missing not at random: whether a value is missing depends on the missing value itself, even after allowing for the recorded variables.

B02 · Missing values

Modified z-score

A robust z-score, 0.6745 × (x − median) ÷ MAD; absolute values above 3.5 are flagged as potential outliers.

B02 · Outliers and impossible values

Monetary unit assumption

The assumption that only events measurable in money are recorded, in a currency treated as a stable unit, so changes in its purchasing power are ignored.

F01 · What accounting is and why businesses need it

Multiple imputation

Creating several completed copies of a dataset with different plausible fills, analysing each, and pooling the results so that the uncertainty about the missing values is reflected in the answer.

B02 · Missing values

N

Near duplicate

A record that describes the same entity as another but whose values differ slightly, for example in case, spacing, spelling or a second email address.

B02 · Duplicate records

Net assets

Assets minus liabilities; another name for equity.

F01 · The accounting equation

Net income

Revenue minus expenses for a period. A negative result is a net loss.

F01 · The accounting equation

Nominal variable

A categorical variable whose categories have no natural order, such as region or product. Supports equality, counts and the mode.

B02 · Datasets: rows, columns, records and variables

Normal balance

The side on which an account records increases, and so the side on which its balance usually sits: debit for assets, expenses and dividends; credit for liabilities, equity, revenue and contra-asset accounts.

F01 · Debits, credits and normal balances

O

Observation

One row of a dataset: all the values recorded for one instance of the unit of observation. Also called a record.

B02 · Datasets: rows, columns, records and variables

Ordinal variable

A categorical variable whose categories have a meaningful order but no fixed distance between them, such as a 1–5 rating. Also supports the median and percentiles.

B02 · Datasets: rows, columns, records and variables

Outlier

An observation that lies an abnormal distance from the other values in a dataset; it may be an error or a genuine extreme.

B02 · Outliers and impossible values

P

Pairwise deletion

Computing each statistic from all rows that have the values it needs, so different statistics use different rows; also called available-case analysis.

B02 · Missing values

Performance obligation

A promise in a contract to transfer a distinct good or service to a customer. Revenue is recognised as each one is satisfied, at a point in time or over time.

F01 · Accrual versus cash accounting

Pivot

Reshaping from long to wide format: the values of one column become column headers. It needs exactly one value for each pair of row label and column label.

B02 · Transforming and reshaping data

Positioning

The place a business wants its offer to occupy in target customers’ minds, relative to competing offers.

M02 · The marketing mix and customer value

Post-closing trial balance

A trial balance prepared after the closing entries are posted. It contains only permanent accounts and confirms that debits equal credits as the next period begins.

F01 · Closing entries

Posting

Copying each line of a journal entry to the ledger account it names, on the same side, with the same date and amount.

F01 · Posting to the general ledger

Posting reference

A cross-reference written during posting: the journal page in the ledger account, and the account number in the journal, so any amount can be traced in both directions.

F01 · Posting to the general ledger

Prepaid expense

A cost paid in advance for benefits to be received in later periods, such as insurance or rent. It is an asset until it is used up.

F01 · Adjusting entries

Price ceiling

The highest price customers would pay, set by the value they place on the product compared with their alternatives.

M02 · Pricing approaches

Price floor

The lowest price worth accepting, set by costs: in the short run the variable cost per unit; in the long run a price that covers all costs at a realistic volume.

M02 · Pricing approaches

Primary key

A column, or set of columns, whose values identify each row uniquely: no two rows share a value and none is blank.

B02 · Datasets: rows, columns, records and variables

R

Ratio variable

A numerical variable with a true zero meaning “none”, so differences and ratios are both meaningful, such as quantity, price or nights stayed.

B02 · Datasets: rows, columns, records and variables

Relevance

The quality of information that is capable of making a difference to users’ decisions, through predictive value, confirmatory value or both. One of the two fundamental qualitative characteristics.

F01 · What accounting is and why businesses need it

Reshaping

Changing the layout of a table between wide and long format without changing its values.

B02 · Transforming and reshaping data

Residual value

The amount a business expects to receive for an asset at the end of its useful life. Also called salvage value.

F01 · Adjusting entries

Retained earnings

Cumulative net income kept in the business since it began, after dividends.

F01 · The accounting equation

Revenue recognition principle

Revenue is recognised when, or as, the business satisfies a performance obligation by transferring control of a promised good or service to the customer, at the amount it expects to be entitled to (ASC 606, IFRS 15).

F01 · Accrual versus cash accounting

Reversal of entries

An error in which the correct accounts and amount are used but the debit and credit are swapped.

F01 · The trial balance

Robust scaling

Rescaling a variable as (x − median) ÷ interquartile range, so that extreme values have little influence on the scale.

B02 · Normalisation and standardisation

Robust statistic

A summary that a few extreme values can’t move far, such as the median, the interquartile range or the median absolute deviation.

B02 · Outliers and impossible values

Row explosion

The multiplication of rows in a join when a key value appears more than once in the other table. Totals computed from the joined table are inflated.

B02 · Transforming and reshaping data

S

SEC

The Securities and Exchange Commission: the US federal agency that regulates securities markets and has legal authority over the financial reporting of public companies.

F01 · What accounting is and why businesses need it

Slide error

An error in which the decimal point is misplaced, so every digit shifts, such as $350 written as $35. When it makes the totals differ, the difference is a multiple of 9.

F01 · The trial balance

Source document

The original record of a business event, such as an invoice, receipt, bank statement, contract or payroll record, that provides the evidence for a journal entry. Also called an original source.

F01 · Journal entries

Standardisation

Rescaling a variable as (x − mean) ÷ standard deviation, so the training data have mean 0 and standard deviation 1. The result is a z-score.

B02 · Normalisation and standardisation

Straight-line method

A depreciation method that charges the same amount in every full period: (cost − residual value) ÷ useful life.

F01 · Adjusting entries

Subsidiary ledger

A separate ledger giving the detail behind one general-ledger account, such as one account for each customer or each supplier.

F01 · Posting to the general ledger

Survivorship rule

The rule that decides which record in a group of duplicates is kept, or how their fields are combined: for example first, latest update, most complete or merge.

B02 · Duplicate records

T

T-account

A sketch of an account shaped like a letter T, with debits to the left of the vertical line and credits to the right. Used for teaching and analysis; ledgers kept in practice use columns.

F01 · Debits, credits and normal balances

Target market

The group of customers a business chooses to serve with a particular offer.

M02 · The marketing mix and customer value

Target-return pricing

Setting the price that earns a chosen rate of return on the capital invested, at a forecast volume: unit cost + (target return × capital invested) ÷ forecast units.

M02 · Pricing approaches

Tax accounting

The branch of accounting that applies tax law to work out a business’s taxable income and the tax it owes.

F01 · What accounting is and why businesses need it

Test set

Data held back from all fitting and used only to estimate how a model will perform on data it has not seen.

B02 · Normalisation and standardisation

Tidy data

A layout in which each variable forms a column, each observation forms a row and each type of observational unit forms a table (Wickham, 2014).

B02 · Datasets: rows, columns, records and variables

Time period assumption

The assumption that a business’s life can be divided into periods, such as months, quarters and years, for which reports are prepared.

F01 · What accounting is and why businesses need it

Training set

The part of the data used to fit a model and every data-dependent preparation step, such as scaling parameters and encodings.

B02 · Normalisation and standardisation

Transaction analysis

Working out from a source document which accounts a transaction affects, whether each increases or decreases, by how much and on which side, and checking that debits equal credits.

F01 · Journal entries

Transposition error

An error in which two digits of an amount are swapped, such as 1,600 written as 1,060. When it makes the totals differ, the difference is a multiple of 9.

F01 · The trial balance

Trial balance

A list of every general-ledger account with a balance at a given date, each balance in its debit or credit column, with both columns totalled to check that they agree.

F01 · The trial balance

Tukey fences

Limits at Q1 − 1.5 × IQR and Q3 + 1.5 × IQR; values outside them are flagged as potential outliers. Outer fences use 3 × IQR.

B02 · Outliers and impossible values

U

Unadjusted trial balance

A trial balance prepared after the period’s transactions are posted and before the end-of-period adjusting entries.

F01 · The trial balance

Unearned revenue

Cash received for goods or services not yet delivered. It is a liability until the business delivers them.

F01 · The accounting equation

Unit of observation

The kind of thing one row describes, such as an order, an order line, a booking or a student.

B02 · Datasets: rows, columns, records and variables

Useful life

The period over which a business expects to use an asset, in years or units of output.

F01 · Adjusting entries

V

Value proposition

The promise of benefits that gives target customers a reason to choose an offer over the alternatives.

M02 · The marketing mix and customer value

Value-based pricing

Setting a price from the customer’s economic value: the price of the next-best alternative plus the value, positive or negative, of the differences from it.

M02 · Pricing approaches

Variable

One column of a dataset: a characteristic recorded for every observation. Also called a field or an attribute, and in machine learning a feature.

B02 · Datasets: rows, columns, records and variables

Variable cost

A cost incurred for each unit made or sold, so its total rises in proportion to volume: materials, packaging, per-unit fees, delivery.

M02 · Pricing approaches

W

Weighted average

An average in which each value counts in proportion to a weight. The mean of all rows equals the average of the group means weighted by group size.

B02 · Transforming and reshaping data

Wide format

A layout in which the values of one variable, such as the month, are spread across several columns.

B02 · Datasets: rows, columns, records and variables

Winsorising

Replacing values beyond a chosen limit, such as a fence or a percentile, with the limit itself, so that extreme values keep their rows but lose most of their pull.

B02 · Outliers and impossible values

Z

Z-score

The number of standard deviations a value lies from the mean: (x − mean) ÷ s.
