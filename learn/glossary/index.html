Paid, non-personal communication from an identified sponsor through mass or digital media.

A contra-asset account holding the estimated amount of accounts receivable that will not be collected, deducted from Accounts Receivable on the balance sheet. Also called the allowance for credit losses.

Accounting for bad debts by estimating, at each period end, the receivables not expected to be collected, recording that estimate as an expense and an allowance, and later writing off specific accounts against the allowance.

The statement a hypothesis test looks for evidence for, contradicting the null hypothesis. It contains <, > or ≠ and is written H₁ (or Hₐ).

The spreading of the cost of an intangible asset, such as purchased software or a customer contract, over the years it is used. The word is also used for the gradual repayment of a loan’s principal.

A table showing, for each payment on a loan, the interest, the principal repaid and the balance still owed.

A loan repaid by regular payments that each cover the interest due and repay part of the principal, so the balance falls to zero by the last payment.

A method for testing whether several group means are equal by comparing the variation between the group means with the variation within the groups; one-way ANOVA has one categorical factor. Abbreviated ANOVA.

An Excel add-in, loaded from File > Options > Add-ins, that adds statistical tools (Descriptive Statistics, Histogram, Correlation, Regression and others) under Data > Data Analysis. The rate US banks disclose on deposit accounts under the Truth in Savings rules, reflecting the total interest paid on the account including compounding. An effective annual rate. Often shortened to APY.

The document a business issues after its financial year: the financial statements and their notes, and, for larger companies, management’s commentary and the auditor’s report.

A series of equal payments made at equal intervals for a fixed number of periods, such as loan instalments, rent or a regular savings deposit. Also the name of an insurance product that pays an income.

An annuity whose payments are made at the start of each period, so the first payment is made now. Worth an ordinary annuity × (1 + r).

F04 · Annuities

The largest amount a person may commit their organisation to on their own authority; larger purchases need approval from someone more senior or a committee.

The suppliers an organisation has vetted and allows its staff to buy from; a new supplier must usually be approved before it can be used.

M01 · How organisations buy

A lookup that, when no key equals the lookup value, returns the row of the largest key below it (or, in some modes, the smallest key above it); used with band tables sorted by their lower bounds.

Annual percentage rate. In its simple sense, a periodic rate multiplied by the number of periods in a year, ignoring compounding; a US credit card’s APR is calculated this way. Buying and selling the same, or equivalent, cash flows at the same time for different prices, making a profit with no risk and none of one’s own money.

The price at which a dealer will sell a security; also called the offer.

A resource the business controls as a result of past events and that is expected to bring it economic benefits. The FASB defines it as a present right of an entity to an economic benefit.

A person’s lasting evaluation of an object or idea, such as a brand, with the feelings and tendency to act that go with it.

M01 · What influences buying

Rules for dividing credit for a conversion among the marketing touchpoints that preceded it, such as last click, first click, linear or data-driven.

An independent examination of financial statements, after which the auditor gives an opinion on whether they are presented fairly, in all material respects, in accordance with the applicable financial reporting framework.

F01 · What accounting is and why businesses need it

Augmented product

The additional services and benefits offered with the actual product, such as guarantees, installation, support and loyalty schemes.

M02 · Product decisions

Authorised shares

The maximum number of shares of a class that a corporation’s charter allows it to issue.

The constant yearly inflation rate that would produce the same cumulative inflation over n years: (end index ÷ start index)^(1/n) − 1.

Average order value

Revenue divided by the number of orders in a period: the mean value of an order. Abbreviated AOV.

A set of measures proposed by Kaplan and Norton (1992) that adds customer, internal-process and innovation-and-learning perspectives to financial measures.

A large final payment that repays all or most of a loan’s principal at maturity, as on an interest-only loan.

F04 · Loan amortisation

A rush by many depositors to withdraw their money at once, which can bring down even a sound bank because its loans cannot be turned into cash quickly.

F00 · Market participants

Base effect

A large percentage change produced mainly by a small or unusual starting value rather than by a large change in the quantity itself.

B01 · Summarising data: changes, averages and spread

Base period

The date or period whose prices serve as the yardstick for real amounts; a price index is often set to 100 in it.

F04 · Inflation and real returns

Base rate

How common a condition is in the population before any evidence is seen, such as the share of orders that are fraudulent; the prior probability in Bayes’ rule.

B04 · Probability

Baseline

The value a result is compared with to judge whether anything changed, such as the previous period, the same period last year, a target, a comparison group or a forecast.

B01 · Business questions and measurable objectives

Basic earnings per share

Net income less preferred dividends, divided by the weighted average number of common shares outstanding in the period.

F02 · The multi-step income statement

P(A | B) = P(A) P(B | A) ÷ [P(A) P(B | A) + P(A′) P(B | A′)]: how the probability of A changes once evidence B is seen.

B04 · Probability

The variation of the group means around the grand mean, measured by the sum of squares between groups, Σ nᵢ(x̄ᵢ − x̄)².

B04 · One-way analysis of variance (ANOVA)

Bid

The price at which a dealer will buy a security.

F00 · Market participants

Bid-ask spread

The ask minus the bid: the cost of buying and immediately selling, and the dealer’s gross reward for providing liquidity.

F00 · Market participants

One of the intervals a numeric range is divided into for counting. Excel counts a value in a bin when it is above the bin’s lower edge and no more than its upper edge.

A constraint that holds exactly at the optimum, so that the resource it describes is used up; relaxing it could improve the objective.

Replacing a numeric value with the interval (bin) it falls in. Bin edges can be equal-width, equal-frequency (quantiles) or set by a business rule.

B02 · Transforming and reshaping data

Binomial distribution

The distribution of the number of successes in n independent trials, each with the same probability p of success; its mean is np and its standard deviation √(np(1 − p)).

B04 · Discrete distributions

Board of directors

The group elected by a corporation’s shareholders to oversee management: hiring and paying the chief executive, approving strategy and dividends, and overseeing financial reporting.

F00 · Governance and the agency problem

Bond

A debt security that promises interest and repayment of the amount borrowed on stated dates; its holder is a lender to the issuer.

F00 · Financial markets

Bond market

The market for debt securities issued by governments, companies and other bodies.

F00 · Financial markets

Bonferroni correction

A way to control the family-wise error rate when making m comparisons: test each at α ÷ m, or multiply each p-value by m.

B04 · One-way analysis of variance (ANOVA)

Book value

An asset’s cost minus its accumulated depreciation; called the carrying amount under IFRS.

F01 · Adjusting entries

Book value of equity

Shareholders’ equity as recorded in the balance sheet: total assets minus total liabilities, measured by the accounting rules.

F00 · Value and valuation words

Box and whisker chart

A chart of a distribution that draws the first and third quartiles as a box with the median inside it, whiskers for the spread beyond the box, a marker for the mean, and separate points for outliers. Also called a box plot.

B03 · Charts and conditional formatting

Box–Cox transformation

The power transformation (x^λ − 1) ÷ λ, or ln x when λ = 0, with λ estimated from strictly positive data to make them as close to normal as possible.

B02 · Skewed data and transformations

Brand

A name, term, sign, symbol or design, or a combination of them, that identifies a seller’s product and distinguishes it from competitors’.

M02 · Product decisions

Break-even inflation rate

The inflation rate at which an inflation-protected bond and an ordinary bond of the same term give the same return: (1 + nominal yield) ÷ (1 + real yield) − 1.

F04 · Inflation and real returns

Break-even point

The volume (or revenue) at which total contribution equals fixed costs, so profit is zero: fixed costs ÷ contribution margin per unit.

M02 · Pricing approaches

Break-even ROAS

The ROAS at which a campaign’s contribution just covers its cost: 1 ÷ the contribution margin ratio.

M02 · Measuring promotion and bringing the mix together

Broker

A firm that arranges trades for clients as their agent, without owning the securities, and is paid a commission or fee.

F00 · Market participants

Bundling

Selling two or more products together for one price, usually less than the sum of their separate prices. Pure bundling sells only the bundle; mixed bundling also sells the items separately.

M02 · Price, demand and pricing tactics

Business analytics

Analytics applied to the decisions of a business, such as what to sell, at what price, to whom, through which channel and with how much stock.

B01 · The four kinds of analytics

Business buying process

The stages of an organisational purchase: problem recognition, general need description, product specification, supplier search, proposal solicitation, supplier selection, order-routine specification and performance review.

M01 · How organisations buy

Business goal

What a business as a whole aims to achieve, usually in money or size, such as more revenue, more profit or a larger market share.

M01 · Marketing objectives

Business key

The column or columns that identify one real-world thing in the business’s own terms, such as an order number or booking reference.

B02 · Duplicate records

Business market

The organisations that buy goods and services to produce other products, to run their operations or to resell: businesses, resellers, governments and institutions.

M01 · How organisations buy

Business-to-business

Marketing to organisations that buy for their own operations or to resell. Abbreviated B2B.

M01 · What marketing is

Business-to-consumer

Marketing to individuals and households who buy for their own use. Abbreviated B2C.

M01 · What marketing is

Buyer (buying centre)

In a buying centre, the person with formal responsibility for dealing with suppliers and arranging the terms of the purchase.

M01 · How organisations buy

Buying centre

All the people in an organisation who take part in a purchase decision, each in one or more roles; also called the decision-making unit.

M01 · How organisations buy

Buying decision process

The five stages a consumer goes through in a considered purchase: need recognition, information search, evaluation of alternatives, purchase decision and post-purchase behaviour. Routine purchases skip stages.

M01 · How consumers make buying decisions

C

Calculated column

A column of an Excel table filled by one formula, which Excel copies to every row, including rows added later.

B03 · Excel tables and cleaning data

Capital

In finance, the money supplied to a business by investors, as debt or equity, to buy the assets it needs. The assets themselves (machines, buildings) are sometimes called physical capital.

F00 · Capital and liquidity

Capital budgeting

The process a firm uses to decide which long-term investments are worth making, by comparing what they cost with the value of what they are expected to return.

F00 · What finance is: money, time and risk

Capital market

The market for long-term finance: debt maturing in more than a year, and shares.

F00 · Financial markets

Capital structure

The mix of debt and equity a business uses to finance its assets.

F00 · Capital and liquidity

Capitalise

To record a cost as an asset, because it will bring benefits beyond the current period, rather than as an expense of the period.

F02 · Long-lived assets

Case normalisation

Converting text to a single case, usually lower case, so that values differing only in capitals compare as equal.

B02 · Text cleaning and consistent categories

Cash basis

A way of keeping accounts that records revenue when cash is received and expenses when cash is paid.

F01 · Accrual versus cash accounting

Cash equivalents

Short-term, highly liquid investments that are readily convertible to known amounts of cash and so near maturity that changes in interest rates barely affect their value: generally those with a maturity of three months or less when acquired, such as Treasury bills and money market funds.

F02 · The statement of cash flows

Cash flow

Cash received or paid out by a person or business over a period, or expected to be received or paid as a result of a decision.

F00 · Finance, accounting and economics

Cash-flow stream

A series of cash flows due at different dates. An even stream (equal amounts at equal intervals for a fixed number of periods) is an annuity; an uneven one is valued by discounting each cash flow separately.

F04 · Discounting cash flows

Cash-flow timeline

A line marked off in equal periods, with time 0 at the valuation date and each cash flow written at the date it occurs, used to set up time-value problems.

F04 · Present and future value

Categorical encoding

Converting the labels of a categorical variable into numbers that a model can use.

B02 · Encoding categorical variables

Categorical variable

A variable whose values place each observation in a group, such as channel, product or a survey answer. It is nominal or ordinal.

B01 · Types of data

Category axis

The axis of a column, bar or line chart that places text labels at equal spacing whatever they say, or dates in time order (a date axis); numbers placed on it are treated as labels.

B03 · Charts and conditional formatting

Category consolidation

Merging the variants of a categorical value into the values of a controlled vocabulary, which changes the counts and statistics of each category.

B02 · Text cleaning and consistent categories

Causation

A relationship in which changing one quantity would change another.

B01 · Interpreting results: charts, comparisons and causes

Cell range

A rectangle of cells written as its top-left and bottom-right cells joined by a colon, such as B2:D9; a whole column is written F:F.

B03 · Cells, references and formulas

Cell reference

The address of a cell, its column letter followed by its row number (such as F2), used in a formula to read that cell’s value.

B03 · Cells, references and formulas

Census

A study that observes every member of the population, rather than a sample.

B01 · Collecting data: sources, samples and bias

Central limit theorem

The result that the sampling distribution of the mean of n independent values gets closer to a normal distribution, with mean μ and standard deviation σ ÷ √n, as n grows, whatever the shape of the population (provided its standard deviation is finite).

B04 · Sampling distributions

Certified public accountant

In the United States, an accountant licensed by a state board of accountancy after passing a national exam and meeting the state’s requirements. Often shortened to CPA.

F00 · Finance, accounting and economics

CFO

Chief financial officer: the senior executive responsible for a company’s financial decisions, including investment, financing and working capital, and for its financial reporting.

F00 · Finance, accounting and economics

Change in accounting estimate

A revision of an estimate, such as an asset’s useful life or residual value, because of new information. It is applied from the period of the change onward, without restating earlier periods.

F02 · Long-lived assets

Channel conflict

Disagreement between members of a marketing channel that gets in the way of the channel working. Vertical conflict is between levels; horizontal conflict is between members at the same level.

M02 · Distribution channels and decisions

Channel level

One layer of intermediaries between the producer and the final customer. A direct channel has zero levels.

M02 · Distribution channels and decisions

Chart of accounts

The list of all the accounts a business uses, each with a name and usually a number, arranged by type.

F01 · Accounts, terminology and classifications

Chebyshev’s inequality

For any data set or distribution, at least 1 − 1/k² of the values lie within k standard deviations of the mean (k > 1): at least 75% within two and 88.9% within three.

B04 · Describing distributions

Chi-square distribution

The distribution of the sum of the squares of k independent standard normal variables, k being its degrees of freedom. It is never negative and is skewed to the right, with mean k; chi-square tests use its right tail.

B04 · Proportions and chi-square tests

Chi-square statistic

χ² = Σ(O − E)² ÷ E, the sum over categories or cells of the squared difference between the observed and expected counts divided by the expected count. It is 0 when every count matches its expectation.

B04 · Proportions and chi-square tests

Churn rate

The share of customers at the start of a period who have stopped buying by its end: 1 minus the retention rate.

M01 · Customer value, satisfaction and lifetime value

Circular reference

A formula that refers to its own cell, directly or through other formulas.

B03 · Cells, references and formulas

Classified balance sheet

A balance sheet that groups assets and liabilities into current and noncurrent categories.

F01 · Financial statements from the adjusted trial balance

Cleaning log

The ordered record of every cleaning step: what was done, to which rows and columns, by what rule, and the row count afterwards.

B02 · Data quality reporting and a reproducible workflow

Client concentration

The share of revenue that comes from a business’s largest few clients; a high share means the loss of one client would hurt badly.

B03 · Dashboards that answer questions

Closing entries

Journal entries made at the end of an accounting period that transfer the balances of the revenue, expense and dividend accounts to Retained Earnings, leaving those accounts at zero.

F01 · Closing entries

Cluster sample

A sample drawn by dividing the population into groups (clusters), such as days or branches, choosing some clusters at random and including every member of each chosen cluster.

B04 · Sampling distributions

Code point

The number the Unicode standard assigns to a character, written in the form U+00E9.

B02 · Text cleaning and consistent categories

Coefficient of variation

The standard deviation divided by the mean, usually as a percentage: spread relative to the size of the values. Meaningful only for ratio-scale variables with a positive mean.

B04 · Describing distributions

Cohen’s d

The difference between two means divided by the pooled standard deviation, so that it is measured in standard deviations; 0.2, 0.5 and 0.8 are often called small, medium and large.

B04 · Comparing two means

Colour scale

A conditional format that shades cells along a gradient of two or three colours between the lowest and highest values.

B03 · Charts and conditional formatting

Combo chart

A chart that combines two or more chart types, such as columns and a line, often with a secondary axis.

B03 · Charts and conditional formatting

Commercial bank

A bank that takes deposits, makes loans and provides payment services to households and firms.

F00 · Market participants

Commodity market

The market for raw materials such as oil, metals and crops, traded for immediate delivery (spot) or through futures contracts.

F00 · Financial markets

Common stock

The equity account that records what shareholders have paid the corporation in exchange for its common shares (share capital under IFRS). Some companies split this amount between the shares’ par value and additional paid-in capital, a detail later courses cover.

F01 · Accounts, terminology and classifications

Comparison date

The single date to which amounts due at different dates are moved, by compounding or discounting, before they are compared or added. Also called the focal date.

Winsorising
