B04 · Intermediate · 10 lessons in four modules · about 13.5 hours

Descriptive and Inferential Statistics

Describe data and chance, then reason from samples to populations with confidence intervals, tests for means and proportions, chi-square tests and ANOVA, by hand and in Excel.

Start with lesson 1 Take the diagnostic first

What you will learn

  • Describe a distribution’s centre, spread, position and shape with measures suited to the data, and say which resist outliers and skew.
  • Calculate and interpret probabilities from contingency tables, using conditional probability, independence and Bayes’ rule.
  • Model counts with the binomial and Poisson distributions and measurements with the normal distribution, and check whether a model fits.
  • Explain sampling distributions and the central limit theorem, and calculate and interpret standard errors.
  • Construct and interpret confidence intervals for means and proportions, and plan the sample size a margin of error needs.
  • Carry out and report hypothesis tests for means and proportions (one and two samples, paired), with p-values, Type I and Type II errors and power.
  • Test relationships between categorical variables with chi-square tests, compare several group means with one-way ANOVA, and check each test’s conditions.
  • Report results with intervals and effect sizes, separate statistical from practical significance, allow for multiple comparisons, and say what the evidence can’t show (non-response, self-selection, causation).

How it works

Each lesson teaches the idea from first principles, works examples step by step, lets you explore it in an interactive, then has you practise with feedback. A lesson is complete when you have worked its guided problems and scored 80% on its knowledge check. Mastery is tracked separately and comes back for review before you are likely to forget. Each module ends with a test; the course ends with a project and a final assessment.

Fictional case

Lantern Coffee Co. A fictional coffee business founded by Ana Lim. It starts as a catering and barista-training service, opens its own cafés and an online shop for beans, and plans a canned cold brew. Every name and figure in it is invented for teaching.

Modules and lessons

Module 1 Describing data and chance

  1. 1Describing distributionsBefore you infer anything from data, describe it exactly: its centre, its spread, where a value sits within it and its shape. Learn the notation that separates a population from a sample, why the sample variance divides by n − 1, what z-scores, percentile ranks and the coefficient of variation say, and when the empirical rule or only Chebyshev’s inequality applies.75 min · not started
  2. 2ProbabilityProbability is the language every test and interval in this course is written in. Learn to read joint, marginal and conditional probabilities from a contingency table, to use the addition and multiplication rules, to check whether two events are independent, to draw a tree diagram, and to turn a screen’s accuracy into the probability that a flagged case is real with Bayes’ rule.70 min · not started
  3. 3Discrete distributionsMany business questions are about counts: subscriptions among the next orders, orders on a day, boxes sold at a market. Learn to describe a count with a probability distribution, its expected value and its standard deviation, to recognise the binomial and Poisson distributions and check their conditions, and to calculate their probabilities by hand and with BINOM.DIST and POISSON.DIST.75 min · not started
  4. ✓Module 1 test12 questions on this module. 70% passes.not taken

Module 2 From samples to populations

  1. 4The normal distributionModel a measurement with the normal curve: turn values into z-scores, find the share of bags under a label weight and the machine setting that keeps it below 1%, with NORM.DIST and NORM.INV and by hand. Then check whether the model fits, and see where it fails.85 min · not started
  2. 5Sampling distributionsA sample’s mean or proportion changes from one sample to the next. See how much, through the standard error σ ÷ √n and the central limit theorem, on Lantern’s strongly skewed spring orders; use the normal model for a sample mean, and know when it misleads. Then compare the ways of drawing a sample and the biases that no sample size cures.80 min · not started
  3. 6Confidence intervalsTurn a sample’s mean or proportion into a range for the population’s, with a margin of error that says how far it could be off. Build z and t intervals for a mean and an interval for a proportion, by hand and with CONFIDENCE.NORM and CONFIDENCE.T; say exactly what “95% confident” means; and work out the sample a margin needs.85 min · not started
  4. ✓Module 2 test12 questions on this module. 70% passes.not taken

Module 3 Testing hypotheses

  1. 7Hypothesis testingTurn a business worry into a null and an alternative hypothesis, measure how surprising the data would be if nothing were wrong, and decide with a p-value or a critical value. Test Kilnworth’s 1 kg bags with a z test and a t test and September’s conversion against its target, then weigh Type I and Type II errors, power, practical significance and the traps of running many tests.85 min · not started
  2. 8Comparing two meansCompare two means the way the data were collected: with a paired t test when the same units are measured twice, and with Welch’s t test when the groups are separate. Run both by hand, with T.TEST and with the Analysis ToolPak, put an interval and an effect size on the difference, and check the conditions before trusting any of it.80 min · not started
  3. ✓Module 3 test12 questions on this module. 70% passes.not taken

Module 4 Categories and groups

  1. 9Proportions and chi-square testsCompare two proportions with a z test and a confidence interval, then use the chi-square distribution to test whether counts fit a model and whether two categorical variables are related. Calculate each test by hand and in Excel with CHISQ.TEST, CHISQ.DIST.RT and CHISQ.INV.RT, check its conditions, and say what a result does and doesn’t show.90 min · not started
  2. 10One-way analysis of variance (ANOVA)Compare the means of several groups at once without inflating the chance of a false alarm. Split variation into between- and within-group parts, build the F statistic by hand and with Excel’s Anova: Single Factor, check the conditions, measure the effect with η², and find which groups differ with adjusted comparisons.85 min · not started
  3. ✓Module 4 test11 questions on this module. 70% passes.not taken
