B01 · Introductory · 8 lessons in three modules · about 10 hours

Introduction to Business Analytics

The four kinds of analytics, turning business questions into measurable ones, and reading data sensibly.

Start with lesson 1

What you will learn

  • Distinguish descriptive, diagnostic, predictive and prescriptive analytics and say which a business question needs.
  • Turn a vague business question into measurable objectives, hypotheses and the data needed to test them, using a structured problem-solving approach.
  • Classify data by type and level of measurement, and choose a sensible way to collect data for a question, recognising sampling and measurement bias.
  • Assess a dataset against the dimensions of data quality and say how each problem would distort an answer.
  • Define KPIs with an exact formula, a target and a direction, and calculate rates, ratios, growth and percentage-point changes correctly.
  • Summarise and interpret data with averages, spread and charts, avoiding common traps such as confusing correlation with causation, misleading charts and mixing groups.

Prerequisites

None. The course starts from scratch.

How it works

Each lesson teaches the idea from first principles, works examples step by step, lets you explore it in an interactive, then has you practise with feedback. A lesson is complete when you have worked its guided problems and scored 80% on its knowledge check. Mastery is tracked separately and comes back for review before you are likely to forget. Each module ends with a test; the course ends with a project and a final assessment.

Fictional case

Lantern Coffee Co.. A fictional coffee business founded by Ana Lim. It starts as a catering and barista-training service, opens an online shop for beans, then launches a canned cold brew. Every name and figure in it is invented for teaching.

Modules and lessons

Module 1 What analytics is for

  1. 1The four kinds of analyticsAnalytics turns data into better decisions. Learn the four questions it answers (what happened, why, what will happen and what should we do), what each needs and produces, where machine learning fits, and the five-step process that runs from a business question to a measured result.75 min · not started
  2. 2Business questions and measurable objectivesMost analyses that fail, fail at the first step: the question. Learn to turn a vague worry into a decision-focused question, a SMART objective and testable hypotheses, and to pin down exactly what counts, for whom, over what period and compared with what, before you open any data.75 min · not started

Module 2 Data and where it comes from

  1. 3Types of dataBefore you can analyse data you have to say what kind it is: a table or free text, what one row describes, which columns are categories and which are amounts, how the data runs through time and who collected it. Each answer limits what you can sensibly calculate.70 min · not started
  2. 4Collecting data: sources, samples and biasHow data was collected decides what it can show. Learn the main sources of business data, how a sample stands in for a population, the four biases that most often mislead (sampling, non-response, survivorship and measurement), how to word a survey question, and the basics of consent and privacy.75 min · not started
  3. 5Data quality: is the data fit for the question?Data can be wrong in different ways, and each way distorts a different number. Learn the six dimensions of data quality, how duplicates, gaps, invalid values, inconsistent codes and time zones move a metric, how to judge whether data is good enough for a purpose, and who is responsible for its quality. B02 then teaches the repairs.70 min · not started
  4. ✓Module 2 test11 questions on this module. 70% passes.not taken

Module 3 Measuring and reading results

  1. 6KPIs: measuring what mattersA key performance indicator is a metric with a job: an exact formula, a target, a direction and an owner. Learn to define an online shop’s KPIs precisely, to tell counts from rates and leading from lagging indicators, and to build a balanced set of measures that is hard to game.75 min · not started
  2. 7Summarising data: changes, averages and spreadMost business numbers are summaries: a share, a change, an average, a growth rate. Learn to calculate each one correctly, to say percentage points when you mean them, to choose between the mean and the median, to describe spread, to weight an average properly and to round without misleading.70 min · not started
  3. 8Interpreting results: charts, comparisons and causesNumbers don’t interpret themselves. Learn to read a chart critically, to compare like with like, to tell correlation from causation, to spot Simpson’s paradox and regression to the mean, to diagnose why a KPI moved, and to write a finding that says how sure you are.80 min · not started
  4. ✓Module 3 test12 questions on this module. 70% passes.not taken

Project and final assessment

  1. ✓Project: Lantern’s online-shop reviewReview six months of Lantern’s online shop: frame Ana’s questions, choose and calculate KPIs, find out why September’s orders fell, judge the evidence and recommend what to do and measure next.not taken
