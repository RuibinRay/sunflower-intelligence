B03 · Introductory · 10 lessons in four modules · about 12 hours

Excel for Business Analytics

Formulas, lookups, tables and cleaning, PivotTables, charts, descriptive statistics and regression output, what-if analysis and dashboards in Excel.

Start with lesson 1 Take the diagnostic first

What you will learn

  • Build formulas with the right relative, absolute and mixed references, and lay out a workbook so its inputs, calculations and results are separate and can be checked.
  • Use logical, text, date and conditional functions (IF, IFS, AND, OR, IFERROR, SUMIFS, COUNTIFS, AVERAGEIFS) to answer business questions from transaction data.
  • Retrieve values with XLOOKUP, INDEX and MATCH, and VLOOKUP, choosing exact or approximate matching correctly, and organise data in Excel tables with structured references.
  • Clean imported data with text, date and value functions in a documented, repeatable workflow, and extract sorted, filtered and unique lists with FILTER, SORT and UNIQUE.
  • Summarise data with PivotTables, choose the chart that answers the question and present it honestly, and use conditional formatting to show what matters.
  • Calculate descriptive statistics in Excel and read regression output (coefficients, R², standard error, p-values, confidence intervals) without claiming more than it shows.
  • Model decisions with Goal Seek, data tables, scenarios and Solver, and design a dashboard that answers its audience’s questions.

Prerequisites

  • B01 Introduction to Business Analytics — recommended
  • B02 Data Cleaning and Preparation — recommended

How it works

Each lesson teaches the idea from first principles, works examples step by step, lets you explore it in an interactive, then has you practise with feedback. A lesson is complete when you have worked its guided problems and scored 80% on its knowledge check. Mastery is tracked separately and comes back for review before you are likely to forget. Each module ends with a test; the course ends with a project and a final assessment.

Fictional case

Lantern Coffee Co. A fictional coffee business founded by Ana Lim. It starts as a catering and barista-training service, opens its own cafés and an online shop for beans, and plans a canned cold brew. Every name and figure in it is invented for teaching.

Modules and lessons

Module 1 Formulas that work

  1. 1Cells, references and formulasA spreadsheet is only as trustworthy as its formulas. Learn how Excel reads a formula, how references move when a formula is filled or copied, why a number can look rounded without being rounded, what each error value means, and how to lay out a worksheet that someone else can check.70 min · not started
  2. 2Functions, logic, text and datesExcel’s built-in functions do the heavy lifting: counting exactly what you mean, rounding by a business rule, making decisions with IF, taking text apart and joining it, and calculating with dates. Learn how each one works, where each one trips people up, and how to handle errors without hiding them.65 min · not started
  3. 3Conditional sums, counts and averagesMost business questions about a table of transactions are “how much, how many, or how good, for the rows that meet a condition”. Learn SUMIFS, COUNTIFS, AVERAGEIFS, MAXIFS and SUMPRODUCT, the criteria that make them precise (operators, cell references, wildcards, blanks and date windows), and how to build a summary table with one formula.60 min · not started
  4. ✓Module 1 test12 questions on this module. 70% passes.not taken

Module 2 Finding and summarising data

  1. 4Lookups: VLOOKUP, INDEX and MATCH, XLOOKUPBring a value from one table into another by matching a key: a product’s cost from a price list, a delivery charge from a table of bands. Learn VLOOKUP, INDEX and MATCH, and XLOOKUP, when to match exactly and when approximately, and how to find out why a lookup returns #N/A or, worse, a wrong answer.70 min · not started
  2. 5Excel tables and cleaning dataTurn a range into an Excel table, so that formulas refer to columns by name and grow with the data; sort, filter and validate it; and clean an imported export with TRIM, VALUE, DATE and the dynamic array functions, keeping the raw data untouched and every step on record.70 min · not started
  3. 6PivotTablesSummarise hundreds of rows by dragging fields: order value by month and channel, events by type, each item’s share of a total. Learn how a PivotTable groups and aggregates its source, how to show percentages and months, how to keep it up to date, and when SUMIFS formulas are the better choice.70 min · not started
  4. ✓Module 2 test11 questions on this module. 70% passes.not taken

Module 3 Showing and describing data

  1. 7Charts and conditional formattingA chart or a highlighted cell should answer one question at a glance. Choose the chart from the question, build it honestly in Excel (a title that states the finding, bars from zero, colour that never carries the meaning alone), and use conditional formatting, including formula rules with mixed references, to make the cells that matter stand out.80 min · not started
  2. 8Descriptive statistics and regression in ExcelDescribe a column of numbers with Excel’s statistics functions and the Analysis ToolPak, see what one large order does to the mean and the standard deviation, build a frequency table, and fit a straight line through catering invoices. Then read every line of the ToolPak’s regression output, and say what it does and doesn’t show.85 min · not started
  3. ✓Module 3 test12 questions on this module. 70% passes.not taken

Module 4 Deciding with a spreadsheet

  1. 9What-if analysis: data tables, Goal Seek, scenarios and SolverA well-built model answers questions. Use one to see how a catering job’s contribution responds to guests, hours and costs (data tables and scenarios), to find the price that reaches a target margin (Goal Seek), and to choose the month’s best mix of events and workshops within limited time (Solver).80 min · not started
  2. 10Dashboards that answer questionsA dashboard is one page that tells its readers where things stand. Learn to start from the audience’s questions, give every number a comparison, lay the page out so the most important measure comes first, build it on tables, formulas and PivotTables so it refreshes, and add the documentation and checks that make it trustworthy.70 min · not started
  3. ✓Module 4 test12 questions on this module. 70% passes.not taken

Project and final assessment

  1. ✓Project: Lantern’s summer workbookAnalyse Lantern’s spring shop orders and its 2026 catering events in a spreadsheet: build the formulas and summaries Ana needs, read a regression of catering invoices, find the relaunched shop’s break-even with Goal Seek and a data table, plan July’s stock order with Solver, and recommend what her shop dashboard should show.not taken
  2. ✓Final assessment24 questions across the whole course. 70% passes.not taken

The course is complete when every lesson is complete, every module test is passed, the project is finished and the final assessment is passed.
