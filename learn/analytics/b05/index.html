B05 · Intermediate · 10 lessons in four modules · about 13 hours

Correlation Analysis

Measure how variables move together, test it, and avoid reading causation into it.

Start with lesson 1 Take the diagnostic first

What you will learn

  • Read a scatterplot for direction, form, strength and unusual points, and choose a plot that shows what a single number hides.
  • Calculate and interpret covariance, Pearson’s r, r², Spearman’s ρ and Kendall’s τ, and choose between them for the data and the question.
  • Test a correlation, give a confidence interval for it, judge the sample size needed to detect one, and control for testing many pairs at once.
  • Find what outliers, skew, curved relationships, groups and restricted ranges do to a correlation, and handle each honestly.
  • Build and read a correlation matrix and use partial correlation to ask what remains when a third variable is held fixed.
  • Explain why correlation does not show causation (confounding, reverse causation, trends, selection, chance), and say what evidence would.

Prerequisites

  • B04 Descriptive and Inferential Statistics — required
  • B03 Excel for Business Analytics — recommended

How it works

Each lesson teaches the idea from first principles, works examples step by step, lets you explore it in an interactive, then has you practise with feedback. A lesson is complete when you have worked its guided problems and scored 80% on its knowledge check. Mastery is tracked separately and comes back for review before you are likely to forget. Each module ends with a test; the course ends with a project and a final assessment.

Fictional case

Lantern Coffee Co. A fictional coffee business founded by Ana Lim. It starts as a catering and barista-training service, opens its own cafés and an online shop for beans, and plans a canned cold brew. Every name and figure in it is invented for teaching.

Modules and lessons

Module 1 Seeing relationships

  1. 1ScatterplotsPlot before you calculate. Read a scatterplot for direction, form, strength and unusual points, choose which variable goes on which axis, and make 612 café-days readable when points pile on top of one another, hide their time order, or tell four different stories behind one correlation.80 min · not started
  2. 2Covariance and Pearson’s rTurn a scatterplot into a number. Build covariance from the products of deviations, see why its size depends on the units, divide by the two standard deviations to get Pearson’s r, read r as the average product of z-scores, and interpret r² with care. Then follow footfall to transactions to sales through the cafés’ log.75 min · not started
  3. ✓Module 1 test11 questions on this module. 70% passes.not taken

Module 2 Measuring and testing

  1. 3Rank correlationReplace values by their ranks and measure how consistently one variable rises (or falls) with another, whether or not the path is straight. Calculate Spearman’s ρ and Kendall’s τ-b by hand and in Excel, handle ties, and choose between r, ρ and τ for curves, data-entry slips, ratings and a rainfall column that is zero on most days.80 min · not started
  2. 4Testing a correlationDecide whether a sample correlation is evidence of a real association, put a confidence interval round it with Fisher’s z, and plan how many pairs a study needs. Test rain against footfall at both cafés, see why r = 0.14 is significant with 612 pairs while r = 0.64 is not with 8, and compare the two cafés’ correlations without confusing significance with size.85 min · not started
  3. ✓Module 2 test11 questions on this module. 70% passes.not taken

Module 3 When correlations mislead

  1. 5Outliers and influenceOne wrong digit among 612 days can turn a near-perfect correlation into a middling one. Find the points that move a correlation, measure how much each one matters, and decide what to do with them without deleting what you dislike.75 min · not started
  2. 6Curves, thresholds and restricted rangesPearson’s r measures how close points come to a straight line. Learn what it says, and fails to say, about curves, thresholds and U-shapes, why looking at only part of the range shrinks it, and why noisy measurements do too.85 min · not started
  3. 7Groups and Simpson’s paradoxBusier days go with bigger tickets in each of Lantern’s cafés, yet the two cafés pooled say the opposite. Learn why combining groups can reverse, create or inflate a correlation, why correlations of averages are not correlations of individuals, and what to report.70 min · not started
  4. ✓Module 3 test10 questions on this module. 70% passes.not taken

Module 4 Many variables and causation

  1. 8Correlation matricesPut every pair of measures in one table, read it as a map of what goes with what, spot measures that say the same thing, and test 28 correlations at once without being fooled by the false alarms that many tests produce: Bonferroni, Holm and the Benjamini–Hochberg false discovery rate.80 min · not started
  2. 9Partial correlationAsk what remains of a correlation when a third variable is held fixed: by comparing like with like, by correlating residuals, or with the first-order formula. Then learn which variables to hold fixed (confounders) and which not to (mediators and colliders).70 min · not started
  3. 10Correlation and causationLearn the six ways two variables can be correlated without one causing the other, what kinds of evidence do support a causal claim, and how to write a finding that says exactly as much as the data allow.75 min · not started
  4. ✓Module 4 test11 questions on this module. 70% passes.not taken

Project and final assessment

  1. ✓Project: Does paid social sell beans?Ana wants to double her paid-social budget because spend and sales are correlated. Analyse the online shop’s 52 weeks of 2027: plots, the right correlations, an outage week, ratings and delivery, trends, and what a correlation can and can’t say about her budget. Then recommend how she should find out.not taken
  2. ✓Final assessment24 questions across the whole course. 70% passes.not taken

The course is complete when every lesson is complete, every module test is passed, the project is finished and the final assessment is passed.
