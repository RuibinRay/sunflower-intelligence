B06 · Intermediate · 10 lessons in four modules · about 12.5 hours

Regression Analysis

Fit, read and check linear and logistic regressions, and know what they can and can’t tell you.

Start with lesson 1 Take the diagnostic first

What you will learn

  • Fit a least-squares line, interpret its slope and intercept in context, and judge the fit with residuals, the residual standard error and R², knowing what each can’t show.
  • Test coefficients and the whole model, give confidence intervals, and tell a confidence interval for the mean from a prediction interval for one new case.
  • Build and interpret multiple regressions with numeric, categorical and interaction terms, reading each coefficient with the others held fixed.
  • Compare models with adjusted R², nested F tests and information criteria, recognise multicollinearity, and choose predictors for a purpose rather than by p-value.
  • Check a model with residual plots and tests, and respond to curvature, unequal variance, dependence and influential points with transformations and robust standard errors.
  • Fit and interpret logistic regression for a yes/no outcome: log-odds, odds ratios, predicted probabilities, the likelihood-ratio test and classification at a threshold.

Prerequisites

  • B05 Correlation Analysis — required
  • B03 Excel for Business Analytics — recommended

How it works

Each lesson teaches the idea from first principles, works examples step by step, lets you explore it in an interactive, then has you practise with feedback. A lesson is complete when you have worked its guided problems and scored 80% on its knowledge check. Mastery is tracked separately and comes back for review before you are likely to forget. Each module ends with a test; the course ends with a project and a final assessment.

Fictional case

Lantern Coffee Co. A fictional coffee business founded by Ana Lim. It starts as a catering and barista-training service, opens its own cafés and an online shop for beans, and plans a canned cold brew. Every name and figure in it is invented for teaching.

Modules and lessons

Module 1 Fitting a line

  1. 1The least-squares lineTurn a scatterplot into a line you can read: the least-squares criterion, the slope b₁ = r·s_y ÷ s_x and the intercept b₀ = ȳ − b₁x̄. Find what an extra transaction is worth at each café, see why Harbourside’s line crosses zero at −$76.58 and why that number means nothing, and meet the two facts that surprise everyone: the line of y on x is not the line of x on y, and extreme days are followed by ordinary ones.85 min · not started
  2. 2Residuals and R²Judge how well a line fits: residuals, the split of the total variation into SST = SSR + SSE, R² as the share of variation the line accounts for, and the residual standard error in y’s own units. Read a first residual plot, see a line with R² = 0.88 that is the wrong shape, and learn three things R² can’t tell you.75 min · not started
  3. ✓Module 1 test11 questions on this module. 70% passes.not taken

Module 2 Inference and prediction

  1. 3Inference for coefficientsDecide whether a fitted slope is evidence of a real relationship and how precisely it is known: the population model and its conditions, the standard error of a slope, t tests and confidence intervals for slope and intercept, the F test, and the ToolPak’s output. Then the two judgements software can’t make for you: whether a significant slope matters, and whether the rows were independent enough to trust its standard error.80 min · not started
  2. 4Prediction and intervalsUse a fitted line to predict, and say how far to trust the prediction: the confidence interval for the average at a given x against the prediction interval for one new case, why both widen away from the mean of x, why the prediction interval never shrinks below the residual spread, and why extrapolation escapes them both. Then roster a Saturday’s peak from a weather forecast, by hand and in Excel.75 min · not started
  3. ✓Module 2 test11 questions on this module. 70% passes.not taken

Module 3 Multiple regression

  1. 5Multiple regressionFit one equation with several predictors, read each slope as a comparison with the others held fixed, and see why a slope changes when a predictor joins the model. Then judge the whole model with adjusted R² and the overall F test, and read the output in Excel.75 min · not started
  2. 6Categorical predictors and interactionsPut categories such as the café or the day of the week into a regression with dummy variables, read each coefficient against a reference level, and let one predictor’s effect depend on another with an interaction. Then see a café dummy resolve B05’s Simpson’s paradox.65 min · not started
  3. 7Multicollinearity and model choiceSee what happens when two predictors carry nearly the same information, measure it with the variance inflation factor, and decide what to do about it. Then choose between models with adjusted R², the nested F test, AIC and BIC, and check a model on data it hasn’t seen.70 min · not started
  4. ✓Module 3 test11 questions on this module. 70% passes.not taken

Module 4 Checking and extending a model

  1. 8Assumptions and diagnosticsA regression’s coefficients, p-values and intervals are only as good as the assumptions behind them. Read residual plots and two standard tests to check each assumption, and learn which failures bias the coefficients and which only distort the standard errors.75 min · not started
  2. 9Transformations and influenceWhen the diagnostics fail, change the model, not the data. Fit footfall in logs and read its slopes as percentages, bend a line with polynomial and hinge terms, and find the few rows that move a regression, then decide what to do with them honestly.80 min · not started
  3. 10Logistic regressionWhen the outcome is yes or no, a straight line predicts impossible probabilities. Model the log-odds instead: fit and read a logistic regression, turn its coefficients into odds ratios and probabilities, test them, and judge a classification against the base rate.70 min · not started
  4. ✓Module 4 test10 questions on this module. 70% passes.not taken
