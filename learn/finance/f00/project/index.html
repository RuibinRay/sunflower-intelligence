F00 · Applied project

Two choices for Ana’s savings

Fictional case

It is February 2026. On 1 March Ana Lim will put $20,000 of her $26,000 savings into Lantern Coffee Co. and give up her $52,000 salary to run it. She wants to keep the other $6,000 as an emergency fund for the next two years, in case the business is slow to pay her or something goes wrong at home. Today it sits in a Coastline Bank savings account paying 1.5% a year. She has narrowed the choice to two accounts and asked you which to use, and why, in words she can follow. The banks, rates and Ana are fictional.

Ana’s two choices (fictional banks and rates)
Two-year certificate of depositHigh-yield savings account
BankCoastline BankSandpiper Online
Rate4.0% a year, fixed for two years3.2% a year, variable
Interest paidOnce a year, added to the balanceMonthly, added to the balance
AccessLocked for two years; early withdrawal costs six months’ interestWithdraw any amount at any time
Deposit insuranceFDIC-insured bankFDIC-insured bank

Ana assumes inflation of 2.8% a year for the next two years. That is her working assumption, not a forecast. You will compare the choices on the four ideas this course is built on: time value (how much each grows), inflation (what the growth is worth in purchasing power), liquidity (what happens if she needs the money early) and risk (what could change). Then you will write her a short recommendation.

Note: Deposit insurance

In the United States, FDIC deposit insurance covers $250,000 per depositor, per FDIC-insured bank, for each account ownership category, and it covers savings accounts and certificates of deposit (FDIC, “Understanding Deposit Insurance”). Ana’s $6,000 is well inside the limit at either bank, so the risk of losing the money if a bank fails is not what separates the two choices.

What you will produce

  • The value of $6,000 after two years in each account, and the effective annual rate of the savings account.
  • The real (after-inflation) return of each account and of Ana’s current account, and what the certificate’s balance is worth in today’s dollars.
  • What Ana would get from each account if she needed all the money after one year.
  • A recommendation to Ana of about 200–300 words that names the choice, gives the numbers that matter and says what would change your answer.
