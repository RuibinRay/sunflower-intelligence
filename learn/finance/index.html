Record transactions, prepare and read financial statements, value money over time, and make investment and financing decisions. Courses are listed in the order to take them, from introductory to advanced; each lists what it requires. Courses marked Planned are not written yet, but their syllabus is here and some link to plates and tools that already cover part of the ground.

Suggested order

If you are starting out, take the courses in this order: each comes after everything it requires, and the general before the specialised. Step 1, F00, is open now and starts from scratch.

Introductory

  • F00Complete

    Foundations of Finance

    What finance is and how it differs from accounting and economics, the principles behind every financial decision, the vocabulary professionals use, and how financial markets and their participants work.

    Introductory · 9 lessons · about 11 hours

    Open the course

  • F01Complete

    Introduction to Financial Accounting

    How a business records what happens to it: the accounting equation, debits and credits, journals and ledgers, adjustments, and the full accounting cycle.

    Introductory · 12 lessons · about 16 hours · F00 recommended

    Open the course

  • F02Complete

    Financial Statement Preparation

    Build all four financial statements and see how every transaction flows through them.

    Introductory · 10 lessons · about 14.5 hours · requires F01

    Open the course

  • F04Complete

    Mathematics of Finance

    Interest, present and future value, annuities, loans and inflation, with every formula worked step by step.

    Introductory · 9 lessons · about 12.5 hours · F00 recommended

    Open the course

Intermediate

  • F03Complete

    Financial Statement Analysis

    Read a company through its statements: trends, common-size statements, ratios, DuPont analysis and distress scores.

    Intermediate · 10 lessons · about 12 hours · requires F02 · F04 recommended

    Open the course

  • F05Complete

    Corporate Finance

    Which projects to fund, what capital costs, how to finance the business and what to pay shareholders.

    Intermediate · 10 lessons · about 13 hours · requires F04 · F03 recommended

    Open the course

  • F12Planned

    Managerial Accounting and Control

    The numbers managers use inside the business: cost behaviour, product costing, budgets, variances and performance measures that steer decisions.

    Intermediate · requires F02 · F03 recommended

    What it will cover
    • Financial versus managerial accounting
    • Cost behaviour: fixed, variable and mixed costs
    • Cost–volume–profit analysis and break-even
    • Job-order and process costing
    • Activity-based costing
    • Relevant costs for decisions: make or buy, special orders, dropping a product
    • Budgets and the master budget
    • Flexible budgets and variance analysis
    • Standard costing
    • Responsibility centres, transfer pricing and return on investment
    • Balanced scorecard and management control systems
  • F06Reviewed

    Investment Analysis

    Risk and return, diversification, CAPM, bonds and equity valuation.

    Intermediate · 11 lessons · about 14 hours · requires F04 · B04 recommended

    Open the course

  • F13Planned

    Financial Markets, Institutions and Ethics

    How banks, insurers, exchanges and regulators work, why markets are efficient and when they are not, what behavioural finance adds, how currencies are priced, and the ethical standards of the profession.

    Intermediate · requires F00 · F06 recommended

    What it will cover
    • Banks: deposits, lending, capital and runs
    • Central banks and monetary policy
    • Insurance and pooling risk
    • Exchanges, brokers, dealers and clearing
    • Market efficiency: forms and evidence
    • Behavioural finance: biases and limits to arbitrage
    • Bubbles and financial crises
    • Regulation and its trade-offs
    • Foreign exchange: spot, forward and interest-rate parity
    • Ethics and professional standards: duties to clients, conflicts and misconduct

    Already on this site

    • Bonds and options

Advanced

  • F07In progress

    Financial Modelling

    Build, check and use a three-statement model: history set up from the accounts, forecasts from drivers, working capital, capital spending and debt schedules, a balance sheet that balances, a DCF valuation, scenarios and a model another person can trust.

    Advanced · 9 lessons · about 9 hours · requires F02, F05 · F03, B03 recommended

    Open the course

  • F08Planned

    Derivatives and Risk Management

    Forwards, futures, swaps and options: how they are priced by no arbitrage, and how a business or a portfolio uses them to hedge and to measure risk.

    Advanced · requires F05, F06 · F04 recommended

    What it will cover
    • Why firms manage risk
    • Forwards and futures: payoffs, margin and marking to market
    • Pricing forwards by no arbitrage: cost of carry
    • Interest-rate and currency swaps
    • Options: calls, puts, payoffs and profits
    • Put–call parity
    • Binomial option pricing
    • Black–Scholes–Merton and the Greeks
    • Hedging with futures and options: hedge ratios and basis risk
    • Margin, leverage and short selling
    • Value at Risk and expected shortfall
    • Stress testing and backtesting

    Already on this site

    • Plate: Options and what drives their value
    • Plate: Value at Risk and Expected Shortfall
    • Risk management
    • Bonds and options
  • F09Planned

    Fixed Income and Credit

    Price and analyse bonds beyond the basics: the yield curve, duration and convexity, credit risk and spreads, and the securitised products built from loans.

    Advanced · requires F04, F06

    What it will cover
    • Bond features, indentures and covenants
    • Yield measures: yield to maturity, current yield, yield to call
    • Spot rates, forward rates and the yield curve
    • Theories of the term structure
    • Duration and convexity
    • Immunisation and liability matching
    • Credit risk: default probability, loss given default, expected loss
    • Credit ratings and credit spreads
    • Floating-rate notes and inflation-linked bonds
    • Mortgage-backed and asset-backed securities
    • Bond portfolio strategies

    Already on this site

    • Plate: Bonds, yields and duration
    • Plate: Spotting financial distress
    • Bonds and options
  • F10Planned

    Valuation, Mergers and Restructuring

    Value a whole business with intrinsic and relative methods, then the deals that change companies: acquisitions, leveraged buyouts, distress and restructuring.

    Advanced · requires F05, F06 · F03 recommended

    What it will cover
    • Intrinsic and relative valuation: when each fits
    • Free cash flow to the firm and to equity
    • Terminal value and its traps
    • Valuing young, cyclical and distressed companies
    • Multiples: choosing, adjusting and reading them
    • Sum of the parts
    • Mergers and acquisitions: synergies, premiums and who gains
    • Deal structure: cash or shares, accretion and dilution
    • Leveraged buyouts
    • Financial distress, bankruptcy and restructuring
    • Corporate governance and control in deals

    Already on this site

    • Company Valuation Studio
    • Company Intelligence
  • F11Planned

    Alternative Investments

    Private equity, venture capital, hedge funds, real estate, private credit and commodities: how each makes money, how its returns are measured, and what the reported numbers hide.

    Advanced · requires F06 · F10 recommended

    What it will cover
    • What makes an investment alternative
    • Fund structures: general and limited partners, fees and carried interest
    • Private equity: buyouts and value creation
    • Venture capital: stages, valuation and the power law
    • Measuring private returns: IRR, multiples and public market equivalent
    • Hedge fund strategies and their risks
    • Real estate: income, cap rates and leverage
    • Private credit
    • Commodities and infrastructure
    • Smoothed returns, survivorship and selection bias
    • Due diligence and portfolio allocation
