Record transactions, prepare and read financial statements, value money over time, and make investment and financing decisions. Courses are listed in the order to take them, from introductory to advanced; each lists what it requires. Courses marked Planned are not written yet, but their syllabus is here and some link to plates and tools that already cover part of the ground.

Suggested order

If you are starting out, take the courses in this order: each comes after everything it requires, and the general before the specialised. Step 1, F00, is open now and starts from scratch.

  1. 1F00 Foundations of FinanceIntroductory · start here

Introductory

  • F00Complete

    Foundations of Finance

    What finance is and how it differs from accounting and economics, the principles behind every financial decision, the vocabulary professionals use, and how financial markets and their participants work.

    Introductory · 9 lessons · about 11 hours

    Open the course

  • F01Complete

    Introduction to Financial Accounting

    How a business records what happens to it: the accounting equation, debits and credits, journals and ledgers, adjustments, and the full accounting cycle.

    Introductory · 12 lessons · about 16 hours · F00 recommended

    Open the course

  • F02Reviewed

    Financial Statement Preparation

    Build all four financial statements and see how every transaction flows through them.

    Introductory · 10 lessons · about 14.5 hours · requires F01

    Open the course

  • F04Complete

    Mathematics of Finance

    Interest, present and future value, annuities, loans and inflation, with every formula worked step by step.

    Introductory · 9 lessons · about 12.5 hours · F00 recommended

    Open the course

    • Vertical analysis
    • Common-size statements
    • Growth and margin ratios
    • Liquidity ratios: current and quick
    • Solvency and leverage ratios
    • Efficiency ratios: asset and inventory turnover
    • Return on assets and return on equity
    • DuPont analysis
    • Financial health analysis
    • Altman Z-score and its limits
    • Industry benchmarking
    • Reading footnotes and MD&A
    • Cases: growth with cash burn, reinvestment over reported profit, aggressive accounting
