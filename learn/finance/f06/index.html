F06 · Intermediate · 11 lessons in four modules · about 14 hours

Investment Analysis

Risk and return, diversification, CAPM, bonds and equity valuation.

Start with lesson 1 Take the diagnostic first

What you will learn

  • Describe what shares and bonds are, how they trade and are priced, and calculate market capitalisation and enterprise value.
  • Price a bond, calculate its yield to maturity, and use duration and convexity to estimate how its price moves with interest rates and credit risk.
  • Measure returns (holding-period, arithmetic, geometric, real) and risk (standard deviation, drawdowns, the spread of outcomes) from history and from scenarios, and say what each can and can’t tell you.
  • Explain diversification with covariance and correlation, and show how much risk it removes and what it leaves.
  • Build efficient portfolios, combine them with a risk-free asset along the capital allocation line, and choose a mix for an investor’s risk tolerance and horizon.
  • Estimate beta by regression, use the CAPM to find a required return, and judge funds by risk-adjusted performance and alpha.
  • Compare index funds, ETFs and active funds by cost, tracking and evidence on performance.
  • Value a share by discounted cash flow with a terminal value and by multiples, and judge the result with fundamental analysis.

Prerequisites

  • F04 Mathematics of Finance — required
  • B04 Descriptive and Inferential Statistics — recommended

How it works

Each lesson teaches the idea from first principles, works examples step by step, lets you explore it in an interactive, then has you practise with feedback. A lesson is complete when you have worked its guided problems and scored 80% on its knowledge check. Mastery is tracked separately and comes back for review before you are likely to forget. Each module ends with a test; the course ends with a project and a final assessment.

Fictional case

Lantern Coffee Co. A fictional coffee business founded by Ana Lim. It starts as a catering and barista-training service, opens its own cafés and an online shop for beans, and plans a canned cold brew. Every name and figure in it is invented for teaching.

The diagnostic has 12 questions across the course. It doesn’t count toward anything; it suggests the module to start from. Take the diagnostic.

Modules and lessons

Module 1 Securities and markets

  1. 1Shares and marketsWhat a share is, how shares are issued and traded, how an index is built, and what market capitalisation and enterprise value measure, using two listed coffee and food companies.80 min · not started
  2. 2Bond prices and yieldsPrice a bond as the present value of its payments, read its yields, measure how far its price moves when interest rates change (duration and convexity), and see what credit risk adds to a yield.80 min · not started
  3. ✓Module 1 test10 questions on this module. 70% passes.not taken

Module 2 Risk and return

  1. 3Measuring returnsTurn prices and payouts into returns you can compare: holding-period returns, arithmetic and geometric averages, annualised and real returns, expected returns from scenarios and the premium over the risk-free rate, using fifteen years of simulated fund returns and Ana’s retirement plan.80 min · not started
  2. 4Measuring riskPut numbers on how far returns can stray: variance and standard deviation from scenarios and history, annualised volatility, what a normal distribution would predict and where real returns break it, worst years and drawdowns, and how the time until you need the money changes what risk means.80 min · not started
  3. ✓Module 2 test12 questions on this module. 70% passes.not taken

Module 3 Portfolios and the market

  1. 5DiversificationMeasure how two investments move together with covariance and correlation, work out the risk of a portfolio, and see how much risk spreading money removes, how much it can’t, and when it lets you down.75 min · not started
  2. 6Efficient portfoliosFind the mixes of funds that give the most expected return for each level of risk, combine the best of them with a safe asset, and choose how much risk to take, while allowing for how little fifteen years of returns really tell you.70 min · not started
  3. 7The CAPM and betaEstimate a share’s beta by regressing its excess returns on the market’s, say how precise the estimate is, use the capital asset pricing model for a required return, and judge funds by alpha, Treynor and Sharpe ratios.65 min · not started
  4. 8Funds and indexingCompare mutual funds, ETFs and index funds; work out what fees cost over decades; judge an active fund against its index with alpha and tracking error; and weigh the evidence on active management before choosing a fund.75 min · not started
  5. ✓Module 3 test12 questions on this module. 70% passes.not taken

Module 4 Valuing shares

  1. 9Discounted cash flow valuationValue a share as the present value of the cash it will pay: dividends discounted at the cost of equity, or free cash flow to the firm discounted at the WACC, with a terminal value and a bridge from enterprise value to a value per share. Then test how much the answer depends on the rate and on growth.80 min · not started
  2. 10Relative valuationValue a share by the price the market puts on similar companies: P/E, EV/EBITDA, EV/EBIT, price-to-book, PEG and dividend yield, why equity and enterprise multiples must never be mixed, how to choose peers, and why two companies in the same trade deserve different multiples.70 min · not started
  3. 11Fundamental analysisRead the business behind a share price: top-down and bottom-up analysis, the ratios that matter for value, quality of earnings, returns on capital against the cost of capital, and a margin of safety. Then weigh whether any of it can beat the market, what the efficient markets hypothesis and its anomalies say, and which of your own biases to watch.70 min · not started
  4. ✓Module 4 test11 questions on this module. 70% passes.not taken

Project and final assessment

  1. ✓Project: Should Ana buy Brightcup?Ana is tempted to put part of her new retirement savings into Brightcup Coffee, the listed chain she competes with. Value the company by discounted cash flow and against its peers, estimate the return its risk requires, and work out what holding it would do to her portfolio.not taken
