F01 · Introductory · 12 lessons in four modules · about 16 hours

Introduction to Financial Accounting

How a business records what happens to it: the accounting equation, debits and credits, journals and ledgers, adjustments, and the full accounting cycle.

Start with lesson 1 Take the diagnostic first

What you will learn

  • Analyse a business transaction into its effect on assets, liabilities and equity.
  • Record transactions as balanced journal entries using the debit and credit rules.
  • Post entries to the general ledger and prepare a trial balance, and know which errors a trial balance cannot catch.
  • Explain the difference between accrual and cash accounting and record the adjusting entries for deferrals, accruals and depreciation.
  • Prepare an income statement, statement of changes in equity and balance sheet from an adjusted trial balance.
  • Close the books and complete the accounting cycle for a small business.

Prerequisites

  • F00 Foundations of Finance — recommended

How it works

Each lesson teaches the idea from first principles, works examples step by step, lets you explore it in an interactive, then has you practise with feedback. A lesson is complete when you have worked its guided problems and scored 80% on its knowledge check. Mastery is tracked separately and comes back for review before you are likely to forget. Each module ends with a test; the course ends with a project and a final assessment.

Fictional case

Lantern Coffee Co. A fictional coffee business founded by Ana Lim. It starts as a catering and barista-training service, opens its own cafés and an online shop for beans, and plans a canned cold brew. Every name and figure in it is invented for teaching.

Modules and lessons

Module 1 The language of business

  1. 1What accounting is and why businesses need itAccounting turns what happens to a business into information that owners, lenders, investors and governments can act on. Learn who relies on that information, the four statements it produces, who writes the rules, and the concepts that make the numbers worth trusting.75 min · not started
  2. 2Accounts, terminology and classificationsAn account is where a business keeps every increase and decrease in one item. Learn the five kinds of account, how accounts are grouped as current or noncurrent and as permanent or temporary, and how a chart of accounts organises them.80 min · not started
  3. 3The accounting equationEvery business’s resources come from creditors or owners, so assets always equal liabilities plus equity. Learn to trace any transaction through that equation and to see how revenue, expenses and dividends change what the owners own.75 min · not started
  4. ✓Module 1 test11 questions on this module. 70% passes.not taken

Module 2 Double-entry recording

  1. 4Debits, credits and normal balancesDebit means the left side of an account and credit means the right, nothing more. Work out from the accounting equation which side increases each kind of account, read normal balances, and calculate the balance of any T-account.85 min · not started
  2. 5Journal entriesEvery entry starts with a document and ends with a balanced record. Decide whether an event belongs in the books, analyse it in five steps, and write it in general journal form, including entries with more than two lines.80 min · not started
  3. 6Posting to the general ledgerThe journal tells the story in date order; the general ledger sorts the same amounts by account so that every balance can be read. Learn to post each journal line, cross-reference the two records so any amount can be traced, keep running balances, and check customer and supplier detail against its control account.85 min · not started
  4. 7The trial balanceList every ledger balance in a debit or a credit column and the two totals should agree. Learn to prepare a trial balance, to use the gap between the totals to track down mistakes, and to recognise the errors that a trial balance in perfect balance can still hide.75 min · not started
  5. ✓Module 2 test12 questions on this module. 70% passes.not taken

Module 3 Accrual accounting and adjustments

  1. 8Accrual versus cash accountingProfit and cash run on different clocks. Learn when revenue and expenses belong to a period under the accrual basis, how that differs from the cash basis, and how deferrals and accruals set up the month-end adjustments.80 min · not started
  2. 9Adjusting entriesSome changes arrive without a transaction: insurance expires, wages build up, equipment wears. Learn to find, calculate and record the five kinds of month-end adjustment, and to prepare the adjusted trial balance that the financial statements are built from.95 min · not started
  3. 10Financial statements from the adjusted trial balanceTurn Lantern’s adjusted trial balance into an income statement, a statement of changes in equity, a classified balance sheet and a statement of cash flows, see how the four fit together, and read what they say about Lantern’s first month.75 min · not started
  4. ✓Module 3 test12 questions on this module. 70% passes.not taken

Module 4 Closing the books

  1. 11Closing entriesAt the end of a period, the revenue, expense and dividend accounts are emptied into Retained Earnings so that the next period starts from zero. Learn the four closing entries, record Lantern’s for March, and prove the result with a post-closing trial balance.80 min · not started
  2. 12The complete accounting cycleEvery period, every business runs the same nine steps, from analysing a transaction to proving that the ledger is ready for the next period. See the whole cycle at once, use the optional worksheet and reversing entries, correct errors after posting, and protect the records with internal control.85 min · not started
  3. ✓Module 4 test10 questions on this module. 70% passes.not taken

Project and final assessment

  1. ✓Project: One month at Brightwater Bike RepairRun a full accounting cycle for a fictional repair shop: journalise a month of transactions, adjust, prepare the statements and close the books.not taken
  2. ✓Final assessment25 questions across the whole course. 70% passes.not taken

The course is complete when every lesson is complete, every module test is passed, the project is finished and the final assessment is passed.
