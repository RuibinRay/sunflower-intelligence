F02 · Introductory · 10 lessons in four modules · about 14.5 hours

Financial Statement Preparation

Build all four financial statements and see how every transaction flows through them.

Start with lesson 1 Take the diagnostic first

What you will learn

  • Apply the five-step revenue recognition model to decide when, and how much, revenue to record, including deferred revenue and refunds.
  • Account for inventory under perpetual and periodic systems, calculate cost of goods sold and ending inventory by FIFO, LIFO and weighted average, and apply the lower of cost and net realisable value.
  • Prepare a multi-step income statement and calculate gross profit, operating income, EBIT and EBITDA.
  • Account for receivables, payables and long-lived assets (bad debts, depreciation, disposals, amortisation), and prepare a classified balance sheet and a statement of changes in equity.
  • Prepare a statement of cash flows by the direct and the indirect method, and trace how a transaction moves through all four statements.
  • Explain the main differences between US GAAP and IFRS for the statements in this course.

Prerequisites

  • F01 Introduction to Financial Accounting — required

How it works

Each lesson teaches the idea from first principles, works examples step by step, lets you explore it in an interactive, then has you practise with feedback. A lesson is complete when you have worked its guided problems and scored 80% on its knowledge check. Mastery is tracked separately and comes back for review before you are likely to forget. Each module ends with a test; the course ends with a project and a final assessment.

Fictional case

Lantern Coffee Co. A fictional coffee business founded by Ana Lim. It starts as a catering and barista-training service, opens its own cafés and an online shop for beans, and plans a canned cold brew. Every name and figure in it is invented for teaching.

Not sure where to start?

The diagnostic has 12 questions across the course. It doesn’t count toward anything; it suggests the module to start from. Take the diagnostic.

Modules and lessons

Module 1 Revenue, inventory and the income statement

  1. 1Revenue recognitionDecide when revenue is earned and how much to record by working through the five steps of ASC 606 and IFRS 15: deposits and prepaid subscriptions, bundles, refunds, discounts and the question of gross or net. Then reconcile Lantern’s online-shop order values with the revenue in its books.85 min · not started
  2. 2Inventory and cost of goods soldFollow the cost of the bags Lantern sells from the roaster’s invoice to the income statement: what goes into inventory, perpetual and periodic records, the FIFO, LIFO and weighted average formulas, the lower of cost and net realisable value, and what each choice does to gross profit, tax and the balance sheet.85 min · not started
  3. 3The multi-step income statementRebuild Lantern’s first-year income statement in stages: net revenue, cost of sales and gross profit, operating expenses and operating income, interest, income tax and net income. Then calculate EBIT and EBITDA, see the rules for presenting non-GAAP measures, and meet earnings per share.80 min · not started
  4. ✓Module 1 test12 questions on this module. 70% passes.not taken

Module 2 The balance sheet

  1. 4Receivables and payablesSelling on credit creates receivables, and some of them will never be paid. Learn the allowance method that US GAAP and IFRS require, estimate bad debts by aging and by a percentage of sales, account for notes receivable, and build up a business’s current liabilities, using Lantern’s first year-end.100 min · not started
  2. 5Long-lived assetsEquipment, vehicles, buildings and patents help a business earn revenue for years, so their cost is spread over those years. Learn what goes into an asset’s cost, how to depreciate it by three methods, what to do when estimates change or the asset is sold, how intangible assets and goodwill are treated, and where US GAAP and IFRS part company.100 min · not started
  3. 6The balance sheet and equityPut Lantern’s year-end balances into a classified balance sheet: decide what is current, split a loan into its current and noncurrent parts, and calculate working capital and the current ratio. Then look inside equity, at par value, additional paid-in capital, dividends and the statement of changes in equity.90 min · not started
  4. ✓Module 2 test12 questions on this module. 70% passes.not taken

Module 3 The statement of cash flows

  1. 7The statement of cash flowsPrepare a full year’s statement of cash flows: decide what counts as cash, classify every receipt and payment under US GAAP and IFRS, work out the direct-method lines from the accrual records, and disclose what changed without any cash moving.75 min · not started
  2. 8The indirect methodStart from net income and reach cash from operating activities: add back noncash expenses, take out gains and losses, adjust for every change in operating working capital, then prepare a full statement from two balance sheets and reconcile it with the direct method.80 min · not started
  3. ✓Module 3 test11 questions on this module. 70% passes.not taken

Module 4 Putting the statements together

  1. 9Linking the statementsPut Lantern’s whole annual report together, check that its four statements articulate and find the error when they don’t, follow single transactions through all of them, see where the gap between profit and cash sits, and push one change through the three main statements the way analysts test a model.80 min · not started
  2. 10US GAAP and IFRSCompare the two frameworks where they differ for the statements in this course (inventory, property, development costs, leases, cash-flow classification and, from 2027, the structure of the income statement), measure what each difference does to the figures, and see why Lantern reports under US GAAP.85 min · not started
  3. ✓Module 4 test12 questions on this module. 70% passes.not taken

Project and final assessment

  1. ✓Project: Brightwater Bike Repair’s first full yearPrepare all four financial statements for Brightwater’s 2027, the year it starts selling bikes and accessories: cost its inventory, estimate bad debts, record a disposal, and show how the statements connect.not taken
  2. ✓Final assessment25 questions across the whole course. 70% passes.not taken

The course is complete when every lesson is complete, every module test is passed, the project is finished and the final assessment is passed.
