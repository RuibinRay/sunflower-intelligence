F05 · Intermediate · 10 lessons in four modules · about 13 hours

Corporate Finance

Which projects to fund, what capital costs, how to finance the business and what to pay shareholders.

Start with lesson 1 Take the diagnostic first

What you will learn

  • Explain why a project is worth taking when its net present value at the opportunity cost of capital is positive, and calculate NPV with stated timing.
  • Calculate and interpret the internal rate of return, modified IRR, payback and profitability index, and say when each misleads.
  • Build a project’s incremental free cash flows: depreciation and its tax shield, working capital, salvage, opportunity costs, side effects, and what to leave out (sunk costs, financing costs).
  • Choose between mutually exclusive projects of different scale, timing and life, and allocate a limited budget.
  • Analyse a project’s risk with sensitivity, scenarios and break-even points, and say what each can and can’t tell you.
  • Estimate the cost of debt and of equity (CAPM with betas from comparable firms, unlevered and relevered) and combine them into a weighted average cost of capital, knowing when the firm’s WACC is the wrong rate for a project.
  • Explain how borrowing changes risk, returns and value (Modigliani–Miller, taxes, financial distress, agency costs), and weigh debt against equity for a real firm.
  • Compare dividends with share buybacks, and plan a firm’s financing with forecasts, the funds a growth plan needs and a cash budget.

Prerequisites

  • F04 Mathematics of Finance — required
  • F03 Financial Statement Analysis — recommended

How it works

Each lesson teaches the idea from first principles, works examples step by step, lets you explore it in an interactive, then has you practise with feedback. A lesson is complete when you have worked its guided problems and scored 80% on its knowledge check. Mastery is tracked separately and comes back for review before you are likely to forget. Each module ends with a test; the course ends with a project and a final assessment.

Fictional case

Lantern Coffee Co. A fictional coffee business founded by Ana Lim. It starts as a catering and barista-training service, opens its own cafés and an online shop for beans, and plans a canned cold brew. Every name and figure in it is invented for teaching.

Not sure where to start?

The diagnostic has 12 questions across the course. It doesn’t count toward anything; it suggests the module to start from. Take the diagnostic.

Modules and lessons

Module 1 Investment decisions

  1. 1Net present valueA firm creates value by investing in projects worth more than they cost. Learn what the opportunity cost of capital is, why accepting projects with a positive net present value makes the owners richer whatever their tastes, how to set out a project’s timing, and how to calculate NPV in Excel for annual and dated cash flows.75 min · not started
  2. 2Investment criteriaManagers and lenders quote a project’s internal rate of return, its payback period and its profitability index as often as its NPV. Learn what each measures, when each agrees with NPV, and the cash-flow patterns that make them mislead: borrowing-type flows, several IRRs or none, and projects of different size.75 min · not started
  3. 3Project cash flowsAn NPV is only as good as the cash flows behind it. Learn to build a project’s free cash flows year by year: the operating cash flow and its depreciation tax shield, working capital tied up and released, capital spending and its after-tax resale, and the costs that do and do not belong, from sunk studies and allocated overheads to lost sales elsewhere in the business.75 min · not started
  4. ✓Module 1 test11 questions on this module. 70% passes.not taken

Module 2 Analysing projects

  1. 4Choosing between projectsWhen projects compete for the same slot, the same money or the same years, the NPV rule needs care. Learn to choose between mutually exclusive projects of different size, timing and life, to compare machines with the equivalent annual cost, and to pick the best set of projects when the budget is fixed.90 min · not started
  2. 5Project riskAn NPV is only as good as the forecasts behind it. Learn to find the assumptions that matter with sensitivity analysis and a tornado chart, to test combinations with scenarios, to calculate accounting, cash and financial break-even volumes, and to see what simulation and real options add, and where they mislead.75 min · not started
  3. ✓Module 2 test11 questions on this module. 70% passes.not taken

Module 3 The cost of capital

  1. 6The cost of debt and equityEstimate what lenders and shareholders require: the cost of debt from yields, after tax; the cost of equity from the CAPM, the dividend growth model and a bond-yield check; and, for a private firm like Lantern, a beta borrowed from listed peers.80 min · not started
  2. 7The weighted average cost of capitalCombine the costs of debt and equity into one rate with market-value or target weights, estimate Lantern’s WACC, and learn when that rate is the wrong one: projects riskier or safer than the firm, flotation costs, and the usual mistakes.80 min · not started
  3. ✓Module 3 test12 questions on this module. 70% passes.not taken

Module 4 Financing and payout

  1. 8Capital structureDoes borrowing make a business more valuable? Work through Modigliani and Miller’s answer without and with taxes, then the costs of financial distress, the trade-off and pecking-order theories, agency costs and covenants, and what finance directors say they actually do.85 min · not started
  2. 9Payout policyHow firms return cash to shareholders, by dividends and share buybacks, why the choice does not change shareholders’ wealth in a perfect market, and why taxes, signals, investor clienteles, agency costs and flexibility make it matter in practice.70 min · not started
  3. 10Financial planningTurn a sales forecast into the money a business will need: percent-of-sales forecasts and additional funds needed, the internal and sustainable growth rates, a monthly cash budget with a line of credit, and scenarios that show what could go wrong.70 min · not started
  4. ✓Module 4 test11 questions on this module. 70% passes.not taken

Project and final assessment

  1. ✓Project: Funding Lantern’s second kitchenAna wants to open a second kitchen in April 2028. Forecast its free cash flows, value it at Lantern’s cost of capital, test what could go wrong, and decide how to pay for it: Coastline’s loan with its covenant, Hollis Avery’s offer for 30% of the company, and whether Lantern can keep paying a dividend.not taken
  2. ✓Final assessment24 questions across the whole course. 70% passes.not taken

The course is complete when every lesson is complete, every module test is passed, the project is finished and the final assessment is passed.
