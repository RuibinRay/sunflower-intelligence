F08 · Advanced · 11 lessons in three modules · about 11 hours

Derivatives and Risk Management

Forwards, futures, swaps and options: how they are priced by no arbitrage, and how a business or a portfolio uses them to hedge and to measure risk.

Start with lesson 1 Take the diagnostic first

What you will learn

  • Measure a business’s exposures to commodity prices, exchange rates and interest rates, and explain when hedging them adds value.
  • Describe how forwards, futures and swaps work, including margin and daily settlement, and calculate their payoffs and values.
  • Price forwards, currency forwards and interest-rate swaps by no arbitrage, and explain the trades that enforce those prices.
  • Calculate option payoffs and profits, apply put–call parity, and price options with binomial trees and the Black–Scholes–Merton model.
  • Design a hedge with futures or options, choosing the instrument and the hedge ratio, and explain the basis risk and leverage that remain.
  • Measure the risk left in a business or portfolio with Value at Risk, expected shortfall and stress tests, and judge whether the measures can be trusted.

How it works

Each lesson teaches the idea from first principles, works examples step by step, lets you explore it in an interactive, then has you practise with feedback. A lesson is complete when you have worked its guided problems and scored 80% on its knowledge check. Mastery is tracked separately and comes back for review before you are likely to forget. Each module ends with a test; the course ends with a project and a final assessment.

Fictional case

Lantern Coffee Co. A fictional coffee business founded by Ana Lim. It starts as a catering and barista-training service, opens its own cafés and an online shop for beans, and plans a canned cold brew. Every name and figure in it is invented for teaching.

Modules and lessons

Module 1 Forwards, futures and swaps

  1. 1Why firms hedgeA business is exposed to prices it does not control: what it pays for its materials, the exchange rate on what it buys abroad, the interest rate on its loans. Measure Lantern’s exposure to the price of coffee, see what a fixed-price contract does to it, and work out when removing a risk adds value and when it only costs money.60 min · not started
  2. 2Forwards and futuresA forward fixes a price privately between two parties; a futures contract does the same on an exchange, with the gains and losses settled every day through a margin account. Fix the dollar cost of Lantern’s euro bill with a forward, follow a week of a coffee futures position through its margin account, and compare the two.65 min · not started
  3. 3Pricing by no arbitrageA forward price is not a forecast. It is set by what it costs to buy the asset now and hold it until delivery, because any other price would let a trader make money with no risk. Price forwards on coffee, on a share and on the euro this way, check Coastline’s quote, and value a forward after the market has moved.60 min · not started
  4. 4SwapsA swap exchanges one stream of payments for another: most often a floating interest rate for a fixed one. Follow Brightcup Coffee as it fixes the rate on $200 million of floating-rate debt, work out the fixed rate that makes a new swap fair from the yield curve, and value the swap a year later when rates have risen.65 min · not started

Module 2 Options

  1. 5Calls, puts, payoffs and profitsAn option gives the right, not the obligation, to buy or sell at a fixed price. Draw the payoffs and profits of calls and puts, see how Hollis Avery could protect his Brightcup shares or earn income from them, and compare a price cap on Lantern’s coffee with Kilnworth’s fixed price.65 min · not started
  2. 6Put–call parityA call and a put on the same share, with the same strike and expiry, are tied together: owning a call and a bond pays exactly the same as owning the share and a put. Use the relation to check Brightcup’s option quotes, build one option from the other, spot an arbitrage in a dealer’s price, and find the limits any option price must respect.55 min · not started
  3. 7Binomial pricingPrice an option by building it from the share and a loan. In a world where the share can only go up or down, a mix of shares and borrowing can copy the option exactly, so the option must cost what the mix costs. Price Brightcup’s options in one step and two, value early exercise of a put, and watch the tree converge as the steps shrink.60 min · not started
  4. 8Black–Scholes–Merton and the GreeksAs a binomial tree’s steps shrink, its price becomes a formula: Black–Scholes–Merton. Price Brightcup’s options with it, read what each term means, measure how the price responds to the share, time, volatility and interest rates (the Greeks), hedge a written option with delta, and find the volatility the market’s price implies.60 min · not started
  5. ✓Module 2 test12 questions on this module. 70% passes.not taken

Module 3 Managing risk

  1. 9Hedging with derivativesA hedge rarely matches the exposure perfectly. Work out how many futures contracts minimise the risk when the futures track a related price, measure what the hedge leaves behind, follow basis risk through a grower’s hedge, change a portfolio’s market exposure with index futures, and choose between futures and options.60 min · not started
  2. 10Margin, leverage and short sellingBorrowing to invest magnifies gains and losses, and a broker that lends will demand more cash when prices move the wrong way. Buy Brightcup shares on margin, find the price that brings a margin call, sell short and see why the loss has no limit, and compare the leverage in margin accounts, futures and options.60 min · not started
  3. 11Value at Risk and stress testsAfter the hedges, how much could still be lost? Measure the risk in Hollis Avery’s investments with Value at Risk and expected shortfall, by history and by the normal distribution, test whether the measure would have worked, and ask what the numbers miss with stress tests.60 min · not started
