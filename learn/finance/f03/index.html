F03 · Intermediate · 10 lessons in four modules · about 12 hours

Financial Statement Analysis

Read a company through its statements: trends, common-size statements, ratios, DuPont analysis and distress scores.

Start with lesson 1

What you will learn

  • Compare two years of statements by horizontal and vertical analysis, with growth rates, common-size statements and margins, and adjust for periods of different length and changes in the business.
  • Measure liquidity, solvency and efficiency with the standard ratios, choose and state the conventions behind each (averages, credit sales, leases as debt), and explain what each one can and cannot show.
  • Calculate return on assets and return on equity and break return on equity into its drivers with the three- and five-step DuPont analysis.
  • Judge the quality of earnings from the cash-flow statement and the notes: cash from operations against net income, accruals, free cash flow and the red flags of aggressive accounting.
  • Calculate Altman’s Z-scores, place a business in its zone, and explain the limits of the model for small, private and lease-heavy businesses.
  • Benchmark a business against peers and published filings, read the MD&A and the notes of an annual report, and write an analysis that reaches a conclusion a lender or owner can act on.

Prerequisites

  • F02 Financial Statement Preparation — required
  • F04 Mathematics of Finance — recommended

How it works

Each lesson teaches the idea from first principles, works examples step by step, lets you explore it in an interactive, then has you practise with feedback. A lesson is complete when you have worked its guided problems and scored 80% on its knowledge check. Mastery is tracked separately and comes back for review before you are likely to forget. Each module ends with a test; the course ends with a project and a final assessment.

Fictional case

Lantern Coffee Co. A fictional coffee business founded by Ana Lim. It starts as a catering and barista-training service, opens its own cafés and an online shop for beans, and plans a canned cold brew. Every name and figure in it is invented for teaching.

Modules and lessons

Module 1 Comparing statements

  1. 1Horizontal analysisPut two or more years of statements side by side, measure what changed in dollars, percentages, index numbers and compound growth rates, and decide which changes are real, which are produced by the calendar or by a new kind of business, and which matter.75 min · not started
  2. 2Common-size statementsExpress every line of the income statement as a share of revenue and every line of the balance sheet as a share of total assets, read the margins that result, and compare a small business with one thousands of times its size without being misled by how each one classifies its costs.75 min · not started
  3. ✓Module 1 test12 questions on this module. 70% passes.not taken

Module 2 Liquidity, solvency and efficiency

  1. 3LiquidityMeasure whether a business can pay what falls due in the next year: working capital and the current, quick, cash and operating cash flow ratios, what belongs in each and why, and how to explain a fall like Lantern’s from 2.15 to 0.52.70 min · not started
  2. 4Solvency and leverageMeasure how far a business is financed by borrowing and how easily it carries the cost: debt and leverage ratios, interest and EBITDA coverage, net debt to EBITDA, leases and vendor finance as debt, and what to do when equity turns negative.75 min · not started
  3. 5Efficiency and working capitalMeasure how hard a business makes its assets work and how long its cash is tied up: asset turnover, receivables, inventory and payables days, the operating and cash conversion cycles, the contract asset as revenue waiting to be billed, and why growth eats cash.70 min · not started
  4. ✓Module 2 test11 questions on this module. 70% passes.not taken

Module 3 Returns

  1. 6Return on assets and equityMeasure how much profit a business earns on the resources it uses and on the owners’ capital, on averages stated openly, and judge when a high return on equity reflects good business and when it reflects borrowing, an unpaid owner or tiny equity.70 min · not started
  2. 7DuPont analysisSplit return on equity into margin, asset turnover and leverage, in three steps and in five, so that you can say why returns moved, compare businesses built in different ways, and see what the decomposition hides.80 min · not started
  3. ✓Module 3 test11 questions on this module. 70% passes.not taken

Module 4 Judging financial health

  1. 8Cash flow and earnings qualityTest how much of a profit has arrived as cash: compare cash from operations with net income, measure accruals and free cash flow, find the estimates inside earnings, and recognise the warning signs of aggressive accounting.75 min · not started
  2. 9Distress scoresCombine liquidity, accumulated profit, earning power and leverage into one score with Altman’s Z-score models, place a business in its zone, and judge when the score can and cannot be trusted.65 min · not started
  3. 10Benchmarking and annual reportsJudge a company’s ratios against the right peers, read an annual report for what the ratios can’t show (the MD&A, the auditor’s report and the notes on revenue, leases and debt), and write an analysis that ends in a conclusion someone can act on.65 min · not started
  4. ✓Module 4 test11 questions on this module. 70% passes.not taken
