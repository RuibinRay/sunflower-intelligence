M04 · Intermediate · 10 lessons in four modules · about 13 hours

Market Research

Plan research around a decision, use secondary sources and qualitative work, design questionnaires and samples, size markets, research competitors, and turn survey data into honest insight.

Start with lesson 1 Take the diagnostic first

What you will learn

  • Turn a business decision into research objectives and questions, choose an exploratory, descriptive or causal design, and write a research brief that weighs the cost of research against what the decision is worth.
  • Find, evaluate and combine internal and external secondary data, judging its source, definitions, coverage and timeliness.
  • Plan depth interviews and focus groups, and analyse what people say with a codebook, while knowing what qualitative research can’t show.
  • Design questionnaires that measure what they intend, avoiding leading, loaded and double-barrelled questions, order effects and response styles.
  • Choose a probability or non-probability sampling design, allocate a stratified sample, and estimate results with their margin of error.
  • Diagnose survey error (coverage, sampling, non-response and measurement), calculate response rates, and weight responses, knowing what weighting can’t fix.
  • Size a market from the top down and the bottom up (TAM, SAM and SOM), and research competitors’ offers and prices ethically.
  • Analyse survey data in Excel (frequencies, cross-tabs, Net Promoter Score with its uncertainty, weighted results) and turn findings into insights and recommendations with their limits stated.

Prerequisites

  • M01 Marketing Fundamentals — required
  • B04 Descriptive and Inferential Statistics — required
  • M02 The 4Ps Marketing Mix — recommended

How it works

Each lesson teaches the idea from first principles, works examples step by step, lets you explore it in an interactive, then has you practise with feedback. A lesson is complete when you have worked its guided problems and scored 80% on its knowledge check. Mastery is tracked separately and comes back for review before you are likely to forget. Each module ends with a test; the course ends with a project and a final assessment.

Fictional case

Lantern Coffee Co. A fictional coffee business founded by Ana Lim. It starts as a catering and barista-training service, opens its own cafés and an online shop for beans, and plans a canned cold brew. Every name and figure in it is invented for teaching.

Not sure where to start?

The diagnostic has 12 questions across the course. It doesn’t count toward anything; it suggests the module to start from. Take the diagnostic.

Modules and lessons

Module 1 Planning research

  1. 1The research processResearch earns its cost only through a decision. Learn to turn a business decision into a research problem and measurable objectives, choose an exploratory, descriptive or causal design, write a brief and read a proposal, put a ceiling on what research is worth, and follow the professional codes that protect the people you study.80 min · not started
  2. 2Secondary researchBefore collecting anything new, find out what is already known. Learn to search a business’s own records and outside sources (official statistics, trade associations, syndicated and panel data, company filings, digital traces), judge each source by who made it and why, its method, definitions, coverage, timeliness and consistency, and combine sources so that each checks the others.70 min · not started
  3. 3Qualitative researchSurveys count; qualitative research explains. Learn when depth interviews, focus groups and observation fit, how to recruit with a screener and write a discussion guide, how many interviews are enough, and how to code transcripts into themes with a codebook, check that two coders agree and report what people said without turning it into numbers it can’t support.75 min · not started
  4. ✓Module 1 test10 questions on this module. 70% passes.not taken

Module 2 Surveys and samples

  1. 4Questionnaire designA survey can only be as good as its questions. Turn research objectives into questions that each earn their place, choose the right question types and rating scales, write options and wording that don’t push answers, guard against response styles and order effects, and pretest the result before anyone sees it, and check that multi-item scales are reliable and valid.85 min · not started
  2. 5Sampling methodsWho could be in your survey decides what it can tell you. Define the target population, frame and sampling unit; choose among simple random, systematic, stratified, cluster and multistage designs and know what non-probability samples can and can’t support; allocate a stratified sample and estimate from it with a standard error; and size a sample for the subgroups you need to read.70 min · not started
  3. 6Survey errorA margin of error measures one kind of error. Learn the others (coverage, non-response, measurement, processing) as one system, calculate response rates by AAPOR’s definitions, see why a response rate is not a measure of bias, weight responses back to the population and count what weighting costs, and read stated intentions for what they are.80 min · not started
  4. ✓Module 2 test12 questions on this module. 70% passes.not taken

Module 3 Markets and competitors

  1. 7Market sizingHow big is the market, and how much of it can you win? Define a market by its products, customers, place and period; separate TAM, SAM and SOM; estimate from the top down and the bottom up; use survey intent with an explicit conversion assumption; and report a range with the assumption that drives it, not one hopeful number.80 min · not started
  2. 8Competitor researchFind out what rivals offer, charge and plan, from sources you can use honestly: websites, price lists, reviews, job advertisements, trade shows, filings, customers and store checks. Run a shelf-price audit and summarise it by brand and channel, know where the professional codes and the law draw the line on mystery shopping and confidential information, and turn the findings into decisions about your own offer.70 min · not started
  3. ✓Module 3 test11 questions on this module. 70% passes.not taken

Module 4 From data to insight

  1. 9Analysing survey dataPrepare a survey export for analysis, then build frequency tables, cross-tabs, top-box scores and the Net Promoter Score with its margin of error in Excel. Weight results to the population with SUMPRODUCT, compare groups with B04’s tests, choose the median for skewed counts, and know when a base is too small to report.95 min · not started
  2. 10Insights and reportingTurn findings into insights and recommendations by combining survey, qualitative and secondary evidence; say what research can’t show and when only an experiment will do; and write a research report with honest charts, uncertainty in plain words and respondents kept anonymous.75 min · not started
  3. ✓Module 4 test10 questions on this module. 70% passes.not taken

Project and final assessment

  1. ✓Project: Office coffee for Harbour CityResearch whether Lantern should launch an office-coffee subscription: set the objectives, fix the questionnaire, sample the business directory, analyse the office survey, size the market from the top down and the bottom up, read the competition, code the interviews and write Ana a memo that separates what the research shows from what it can’t.not taken
  2. ✓Final assessment24 questions across the whole course. 70% passes.not taken

The course is complete when every lesson is complete, every module test is passed, the project is finished and the final assessment is passed.
