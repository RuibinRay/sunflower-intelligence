M06 · Applied project

Keeping Lantern’s coffee subscribers

Fictional case

January 2028. Lantern’s online shop has sold coffee subscriptions since it opened in March 2026: one delivery a month, on the same date each month (the 28th at the latest), at 90% of list price. 261 subscriptions have started. In July–September 2026 the relaunch offered half price on the first month, and 95 people joined on it; others joined at the shop, by a friend’s referral (each side gets $10 of shop credit) or, from March 2027, at the cafés. 120 were subscribed on 31 December 2026 and 168 a year later. From 1 March 2027, a subscriber who asked to cancel was randomly either cancelled, as before, or offered to skip the next month instead. Every subscription is in the register below with its start, how it joined, its plan and price, and the date it asked to cancel if it did. (Some subscribers pay a few months ahead, the unearned subscription revenue in F02’s and F03’s accounts; the register counts each month when it is billed and delivered.)

Subscription deliveries ship free (the shop’s $4.95 delivery charge on orders under $35, from B01, applies to one-off orders), so a delivered month earns the price, less the coffee’s cost (B03’s product costs), the $5.20 parcel Lantern pays and card fees of 3.5% of the price. Ana discounts future margins at 10% a year (0.80% a month). She asks: how well does Lantern keep subscribers, does it depend on how they joined, what is a subscriber worth against what one costs to win, and should she keep the skip offer and repeat a half-price launch offer in 2028?

What you will produce

  • 2027’s retention rate for the subscribers Lantern started the year with, calculated with the right denominator, and its monthly equivalent.
  • Survival curves by how subscribers joined, with censoring handled, and what the difference does and doesn’t show.
  • A projection of the relaunch cohorts’ retention, fitted to early months and checked on later ones, against constant retention.
  • Lifetime value per subscriber with margins, discounting and the retention pattern stated, and what acquisition cost each channel carries.
  • The save-offer test’s effect with its uncertainty, read by the arm each request was assigned to.
  • A memo to Ana on the skip offer, launch offers and the plans that don’t pay.
