M06 · Intermediate · 10 lessons in four modules · about 12.5 hours

Customer Analytics and RFM

Score customers by recency, frequency and value, and measure lifetime value, retention and churn.

Start with lesson 1 Take the diagnostic first

What you will learn

  • Turn transaction records into a customer table (recency, frequency, monetary value, tenure) as of a stated date, and tell contractual from noncontractual settings and what “churn” can mean in each.
  • Score customers on recency, frequency and monetary value, explain how cut points, ties and the number of groups change the scores, and read and act on RFM segments, knowing what they can’t show.
  • Build and read cohort tables and retention curves, and separate changes in customers’ behaviour from changes in the mix of customers.
  • Calculate retention and churn rates correctly, estimate survival with censored data (Kaplan–Meier), compare groups, and project contractual retention with a model that allows customers to differ (the shifted-beta-geometric).
  • Predict which customers will lapse from their past behaviour, judge the prediction on later data with lift and gains, and say why a good churn score doesn’t show who a retention offer would change.
  • Calculate customer lifetime value and acquisition cost, payback and the value-to-cost ratio, with stated margins, discounting and timing, and show how assuming every customer is alike biases the answer.
  • Evaluate loyalty and retention strategies with evidence, including randomised tests with a control group, and weigh their cost against the value they add.

Prerequisites

  • M05 Market Segmentation and Positioning — required
  • B02 Data Cleaning and Preparation — recommended
  • B06 Regression Analysis — recommended

How it works

Each lesson teaches the idea from first principles, works examples step by step, lets you explore it in an interactive, then has you practise with feedback. A lesson is complete when you have worked its guided problems and scored 80% on its knowledge check. Mastery is tracked separately and comes back for review before you are likely to forget. Each module ends with a test; the course ends with a project and a final assessment.

Fictional case

Lantern Coffee Co. A fictional coffee business founded by Ana Lim. It starts as a catering and barista-training service, opens its own cafés and an online shop for beans, and plans a canned cold brew. Every name and figure in it is invented for teaching.

Modules and lessons

Module 1 Customer data and RFM

  1. 1Customer metricsTurn a log of purchases into a customer table, one row per customer, as of a stated date: recency, frequency, monetary value and tenure for each customer, and the base’s average order value, repeat rate and active customers. Describe how unequal customers are, tell contractual from noncontractual settings, and clean the data before you trust any of it.80 min · not started
  2. 2RFM scoringScore every customer from 1 to 5 on recency, frequency and monetary value, relative to the rest of the base: set quintile cut points, handle ties, reverse recency, and choose between 125 cells, a combined score and the R × FM grid. See why scores depend on the method, the number of groups and the date.75 min · not started
  3. ✓Module 1 test12 questions on this module. 70% passes.not taken

Module 2 Segments and cohorts

  1. 3RFM segmentsTurn R, F and M scores into named segments on the R × FM grid, size each one in members and in spend, choose what to do with each (and whom to leave alone), and check the segments against what members did next: Lantern’s “Loyal” members turned out to be among the least likely to come back.75 min · not started
  2. 4Cohort analysisGroup customers by when they joined and follow each group month by month: build and read the cohort triangle, choose between share active, number active and revenue per joiner, tell a better cohort from a season or a change in who joined, and avoid the mix effects that make pooled figures mislead.70 min · not started
  3. ✓Module 2 test11 questions on this module. 70% passes.not taken

Module 3 Retention and churn

  1. 5Retention and churn ratesMeasure how many customers a business keeps, with the right denominator and over the right horizon. See why average retention rises with tenure when customers differ, why “churn” must be defined before it can be counted when nobody cancels, and how revenue retention differs from customer retention.80 min · not started
  2. 6Survival analysisMeasure how long customers stay when some of them haven’t left yet. Estimate a survival curve with the Kaplan–Meier method, compare groups with the log-rank test, define lapsing for customers who never cancel, and project contractual retention with a model that lets customers differ.80 min · not started
  3. 7Predicting churnPredict which customers will lapse from what you knew before they did. Set up calibration and holdout periods without leakage, fit a logistic regression and a simpler recency rule, judge the score with the base rate, lift, gains and AUC, and use it to choose whom to contact, knowing that the likeliest to leave are not necessarily the ones an offer would change.70 min · not started
  4. ✓Module 3 test12 questions on this module. 70% passes.not taken

Module 4 Customer value and strategy

  1. 8Customer lifetime valuePut a number on what a customer is worth: historical and predictive value, margin not revenue, the constant-retention formula with its two timing conventions, a finite horizon, why one average retention rate understates value when customers differ, and a simple projection for members who never tell you they have left.75 min · not started
  2. 9Acquisition cost and paybackWhat does it cost to win a customer, and how long until they pay it back? Measure acquisition cost with the right costs, customers and period, blended, paid and by channel; work out payback with and without retention and discounting; read cohort payback curves; and judge the value-to-cost ratio without treating a rule of thumb as a law.75 min · not started
  3. 10Loyalty and retention strategyLoyalty programmes, win-back offers and other ways of keeping customers: what the published evidence shows, why self-selected members can’t tell you whether a programme works, how a randomised holdout measures what an offer adds, and how to weigh its cost against the value of the customers it keeps.75 min · not started
  4. ✓Module 4 test12 questions on this module. 70% passes.not taken

Project and final assessment

  1. ✓Project: Keeping Lantern’s coffee subscribersEvery coffee subscription since Lantern opened, and a test of offering to skip a month instead of cancelling. Measure retention by cohort and by how subscribers joined, project it, value a subscriber against what one costs to win, and decide whether the save offer and the 2026 relaunch offer were worth it.not taken
  2. ✓Final assessment24 questions across the whole course. 70% passes.not taken

The course is complete when every lesson is complete, every module test is passed, the project is finished and the final assessment is passed.
