Market Segmentation and Positioning

Segment, target and position: find groups of customers who want different things, choose the ones to serve, and decide how to stand out to them, with data rather than hunches.

Start with lesson 1

What you will learn

  • Explain segmentation, targeting and positioning (STP), and judge whether segments are usable: measurable, substantial, accessible, differentiable and actionable.
  • Segment a market by demographic, geographic, psychographic and behavioural bases, and say what each base can and can’t reveal about why people buy.
  • Segment customers on their needs with cluster analysis (standardised variables, k-means and Ward’s method), choosing the number of segments by the elbow, the silhouette and what the segments mean, and checking their stability.
  • Profile, name and size segments with indexes and cross-tabs, test whether they differ, and build a typing tool that assigns new customers to a segment.
  • Evaluate segments’ attractiveness and fit with a weighted scorecard, test how sensitive the choice is to the weights, and choose a targeting strategy.
  • Build buyer personas from segment data rather than imagination, and state their limits.
  • Build and read perceptual maps from attribute ratings, place ideal points, and judge whether a gap on the map is an opportunity.
  • Write a positioning statement with a frame of reference, points of parity and points of difference, choose how to differentiate, and plan a repositioning with its risks.

How it works

Each lesson teaches the idea from first principles, works examples step by step, lets you explore it in an interactive, then has you practise with feedback. A lesson is complete when you have worked its guided problems and scored 80% on its knowledge check. Mastery is tracked separately and comes back for review before you are likely to forget. Each module ends with a test; the course ends with a project and a final assessment.

Fictional case

Lantern Coffee Co. A fictional coffee business founded by Ana Lim. It starts as a catering and barista-training service, opens an online shop for beans, then launches a canned cold brew. Every name and figure in it is invented for teaching.

Modules and lessons

Module 1 Segmentation bases

  1. 1Why segment?A market is rarely one crowd wanting one thing. Learn what a market segment is, how segmentation, targeting and positioning fit together, when segmenting pays and when it costs more than it earns, and how to judge whether a set of segments is usable. Then read Lantern’s needs study to decide whether Harbour City has one coffee market or several.75 min · not started
  2. 2Demographic and geographic segmentationAge, household, income and where people live are the oldest bases for segmenting a market, because they are easy to count and to reach. Learn what each variable captures, how geodemographic systems such as Acorn classify neighbourhoods, and how to test with cross-tabs whether a demographic really separates buyers. Lantern’s study shows why demographics describe who buys far better than why.65 min · not started
  3. 3Psychographic and behavioural segmentationPeople of the same age and postcode can want very different things. Segment instead by how people live and what they value (psychographics), or by what they do and seek: the benefits they want, how much they buy, when and how loyally. Learn why these bases explain choices better than demographics and why they are harder to reach, check the 80/20 claim on Lantern’s heavy coffee drinkers, and use an attitude scale as a segmentation base.70 min · not started
  4. ✓Module 1 test12 questions on this module. 70% passes.not taken

Module 2 Segmenting with data

  1. 4Cluster analysisSegment customers on their needs with data: choose and standardise the variables, run k-means (and Ward’s method as a check), pick the number of segments from the elbow, the silhouette and what the segments mean, and test whether the answer is stable. Lantern’s 640-person needs study is the data.95 min · not started
  2. 5Profiling segmentsTurn a cluster solution into segments a business can use: profile them with indexes against the average, name them honestly, describe who they are and what they buy with cross-tabs and B04’s tests, size them in people and in spend, judge what an opt-in panel can say about sizes, and build a typing tool that assigns new customers from a few golden questions.85 min · not started
  3. ✓Module 2 test10 questions on this module. 70% passes.not taken

Module 3 Targeting

  1. 6Evaluating segmentsA segment is worth targeting only if it is attractive and you can win it. Score segments on size, spending, growth, competition and fit with a weighted scorecard, find the weight that would change the winner, and screen out targets that would be wrong to pursue.80 min · not started
  2. 7Targeting strategiesDecide how many segments to serve and how differently: one offer for everyone, a separate offer for each segment, everything on one segment, or offers shaped for places and people. Count what a new offer takes from the old ones, and build a buyer persona from segment data that says what it stands for and what it can’t.80 min · not started
  3. ✓Module 3 test11 questions on this module. 70% passes.not taken

Module 4 Positioning

  1. 8Perceptual mapsDraw how customers see competing brands: plot two ratings against each other, or summarise many ratings with principal components. Read the dimensions, the attribute arrows and the segments’ ideal points, measure distances on the map, and judge whether an empty space is an opportunity or empty for a reason.80 min · not started
  2. 9Positioning statementsChoose the frame of reference an offer competes in, the points of parity it must match and the points of difference it can own, and write them into a positioning statement with a reason to believe. Then test the statement against M01’s four tests and with customers in a concept test.70 min · not started
  3. 10Differentiation and repositioningChoose how to make an offer different (product, services, channels, people, image), test which differences are worth promoting, and place the offer among the five value positions. Then plan a repositioning on a perceptual map with fixed axes, work out how far customers’ ratings would have to move, and track the result with a repeated survey.70 min · not started
  4. ✓Module 4 test12 questions on this module. 70% passes.not taken

Project and final assessment

  1. ✓Project: Positioning Lantern’s barista workshopsSegment the people who come to, or might come to, Lantern’s barista workshops, choose which segment to serve, build a persona from the data, map Lantern against a college course, tasting evenings and an online course, and write the positioning statement and the changes that would make it true.not taken
