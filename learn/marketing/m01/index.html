M01 · Introductory · 9 lessons in three modules · about 11 hours

Marketing Fundamentals

What marketing is, how customers decide, and how companies create value for them.

Start with lesson 1 Take the diagnostic first

What you will learn

  • Explain what marketing is and how it creates value through exchange, distinguishing needs, wants and demand.
  • Analyse customer value and satisfaction, and calculate a simple customer lifetime value to judge what a customer is worth.
  • Compare the main business orientations and explain what a market orientation requires in practice.
  • Describe how consumers and organisations decide what to buy, including the stages, the influences and the roles in a buying centre.
  • Analyse a business’s situation (SWOT, PESTEL, competitors) and set marketing objectives that are specific and measurable.
  • Identify sources of competitive advantage and choose a competitive strategy that fits a business’s strengths and market.

Prerequisites

None. The course starts from scratch.

How it works

Each lesson teaches the idea from first principles, works examples step by step, lets you explore it in an interactive, then has you practise with feedback. A lesson is complete when you have worked its guided problems and scored 80% on its knowledge check. Mastery is tracked separately and comes back for review before you are likely to forget. Each module ends with a test; the course ends with a project and a final assessment.

Fictional case

Lantern Coffee Co.. A fictional coffee business founded by Ana Lim. It starts as a catering and barista-training service, opens an online shop for beans, then launches a canned cold brew. Every name and figure in it is invented for teaching.

Not sure where to start?

The diagnostic has 12 questions across the course. It doesn’t count toward anything; it suggests the module to start from. Take the diagnostic.

Modules and lessons

Module 1 What marketing is

  1. 1What marketing isMarketing is not advertising with a bigger budget. Learn how marketers define it, why a voluntary exchange creates value for both sides, how needs, wants and demand differ, why defining a business by its product is dangerous, and what marketing looks like in consumer, business, service and non-profit settings.75 min · not started
  2. 2Customer value, satisfaction and lifetime valueCustomers choose the offer they judge best value, stay if it meets the expectations they formed, and are worth what they bring in over the years they stay. Learn customer-perceived value, satisfaction and loyalty, how to calculate a simple customer lifetime value with its assumptions stated, how to compare it with the cost of winning a customer, and how to write a value proposition.70 min · not started
  3. 3Market orientation: putting customers at the centreBusinesses differ in what they start from: what they can make, how good the product is, how hard they sell, or what customers need. Learn the five classic orientations and their limits, what a market orientation requires in practice (intelligence about customers and competitors, shared across the business and acted on), how to read what customers tell you, and why ethics and society belong in the picture.75 min · not started
  4. ✓Module 1 test11 questions on this module. 70% passes.not taken

Module 2 How customers buy

  1. 4How consumers make buying decisionsEvery purchase, from a bag of beans to a barista course, comes out of a decision, made in seconds or over weeks. Learn the five stages of the buying process, how involvement changes it, how buyers weigh alternatives (with a scoring model you can calculate), what happens after the sale, and where the tidy model breaks down, online and off.70 min · not started
  2. 5What influences buyingWhy do two people with the same need buy different things? Learn the cultural, social, personal and psychological influences on a purchase; what motivation, perception, learning and attitudes mean; why word of mouth carries so much weight; and how anchoring, defaults and loss aversion shape choices, with the evidence and the ethics of using them.75 min · not started
  3. 6How organisations buyOrganisations buy differently from households: their demand is derived from their own customers’, they are fewer and larger, purchasing is a profession, and several people share each decision. Learn the three buying situations, the six roles in a buying centre, the stages of the business buying process and the procurement rules that decide how a seller must sell.70 min · not started
  4. ✓Module 2 test12 questions on this module. 70% passes.not taken

Module 3 Foundations of strategy

  1. 7Situation analysisBefore deciding where to go, a business needs an honest picture of where it stands. Learn to scan the marketing environment with PESTEL, profile competitors and compare them with numbers, and build a SWOT analysis that rests on evidence and leads to decisions, knowing what each tool can and can’t tell you.75 min · not started
  2. 8Marketing objectivesA business goal says where the business wants to be; a marketing objective says which customers must do what, by when, to get it there. Learn to turn a goal into SMART objectives across the funnel, and to work out how many new customers a growth target really needs once you allow for the customers you lose.70 min · not started
  3. 9Competitive advantageCustomers choose between alternatives, so a business needs a reason to be chosen that rivals can’t easily copy. Learn where competitive advantage comes from (lower cost, differentiation and focus), how to compare competing on price with competing on value, and how to judge whether an advantage will last.70 min · not started
  4. ✓Module 3 test12 questions on this module. 70% passes.not taken

Project and final assessment

  1. ✓Project: Lantern’s marketing foundations for 2027Help Ana plan Lantern’s catering marketing: map how a corporate client buys, value a client, analyse Lantern’s situation, set objectives for 2027 and choose how to compete with a cheaper rival.not taken
  2. ✓Final assessment25 questions across the whole course. 70% passes.not taken

The course is complete when every lesson is complete, every module test is passed, the project is finished and the final assessment is passed.
