Unit economics

Is each new customer worth what it costs to win? Four numbers that investors in subscription businesses check first: lifetime value against acquisition cost, months to earn that cost back, the magic number, and net revenue retention.

    • Lifetime value here is gross profit, not revenue, and assumes churn stays at today’s rate. A stricter measure than the revenue-based figure often quoted.
    • The thresholds are investors’ rules of thumb: lifetime value above three times acquisition cost and the cost earned back within twelve months (Skok), a magic number above about 0.75. Businesses that sell to large companies often run longer paybacks on purpose.

    Planning

    • Decide the sample size before you start and read the result once at the end. Checking every day and stopping at the first p below 0.05 makes false wins far more likely.
    • The test uses the normal approximation, which needs at least about ten conversions and ten non-conversions in each version.
