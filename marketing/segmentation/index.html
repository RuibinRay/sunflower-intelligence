Segmentation, targeting and positioning start here: split a market by what people want, size each group, and decide which to serve. Then, among the customers you already have, see who is warm and who is cooling.

Segment by needs

Groups that want different things need different offers. Here k-means groups survey respondents who answered alike about what matters to them when they buy; the profile then shows what sets each group apart, and how big each one is in people and in spend.

Questions to segment on

Rating questions work best. Each is standardised first, so a 1–7 scale and a 1–10 scale count the same.

5
  • k-means finds the groups the numbers support, not the ones that exist: change the questions or the number of segments and the groups change. Keep a split only if each group is big enough to serve and different enough to need its own offer.
  • Segments are lettered by size, largest first. The names describe the answers and are not personas.

Choose the target

Segmentation pays only when you pick. Score each segment above on its size, how much each person spends, and how well its needs match what you are good at, with weights you set.

  • Size and spend are scored against the best segment (100 = the biggest, or the highest spenders). Fit is 50 for a segment whose needs are average, rising when it cares most about your strengths and least about your weaknesses.
  • The scorecard ranks the segments in this survey. Check that the winner is reachable through channels you can use, and big enough in the real market, before committing.

Customer value (RFM)

RFM scores every customer from 1 to 5 on recency (days since the last order), frequency (number of orders) and monetary value (total spend), then groups them so each group can get the message that fits.

Orders

Defaults to the day after the latest order.

  • Scores are quintiles within this customer base, so a “5” means top fifth here, not an absolute standard. Customers with identical values share a score.
  • RFM looks backwards. Pair it with margins and acquisition cost before deciding where to spend.
